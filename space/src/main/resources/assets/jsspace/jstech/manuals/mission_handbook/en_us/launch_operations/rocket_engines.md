# Rocket Engines

## What it is

The blocks of a rocket's engines, and of the smaller motors on its other parts. An engine is three or five blocks across. Its top layer is the Engine Mount, the Combustion Chamber in the middle, the Turbopump and the Gimbal Actuators in its ring; below are the Nozzle Walls of its bell, and on a vacuum engine a Nozzle Extension under them. Ullage Motors and Retro Rockets stand round a Separation Ring, Cooling Louvres on the Instrument Ring, Thruster Quads round a capsule's docking port and on the lander's cabin. The lunar lander's Descent Engine is at the foot of its descent stage, its Ascent Engine in its cabin.

## What it is for

Lifting the rocket. Both engines burn methalox. The Sea-Level Engine's small nozzle does well in the air; the Vacuum Engine's large one gives more up high and does badly on the ground.

## How to get it

From the creative menu, J's Space tab.

## How to use it

1. On the Rocket Design screen, drag an engine under each stage's tanks. Its spec sheet gives its thrust and specific impulse at sea level and in a vacuum.
2. Fit a Sea-Level Engine to the first stage and Vacuum Engines above it: the screen sums the first burn in air, the later ones in a vacuum.
3. Read each stage's delta-v and thrust against weight before you build.

## What can go wrong

> [!PROBLEM] The screen says Too weak to lift off.
> The first stage pushes no harder than the rocket weighs on this world. Fit a larger engine, or make the rocket lighter.

> [!PROBLEM] The screen says a stage has no propellant.
> It has an engine and nothing to burn. Put tanks over the engine.
