# Mars

## What it is

A cold desert world under a thin air of carbon dioxide, its sky butterscotch and its sunsets blue. Its ground is laid as its sphere is seen from orbit: the low smooth plains of the north and the old cratered highlands of the south on either side of the dichotomy, Tharsis' volcanoes standing and Valles Marineris cut where its heights have them, dark dunes, broken chaos, the beds of old lakes, and caps of ice at its poles. Phobos crosses its sky; Deimos and the Overworld are points of light. Nothing lives there.

![From orbit](jsspace:gui/guide/mars "96x96")

> [!DEFINE] Dichotomy
> The boundary across Mars between its low, smooth north and its high, cratered south.

## What it is for

A crew finds Mars' own ground there, and nowhere else:

- Martian Regolith, the iron-oxide dust that covers most of Mars.
- Hematite Regolith, the ground of the oxide plains, with hematite's grey spherules in it.
- Basalt Sand, the dark sand of the dunes.
- Martian Basalt, broken in fine facets: the bedrock under all of Mars, bare on its steep walls. Where it shows, geology varies it: Columnar Martian Basalt on steep scarps and canyon walls, and Vesicular Martian Basalt, pocked by gas bubbles, on its open tops.
- Martian Basalt Stones, vesicular stones lying loose on the plains and the highlands.
- Mudstone, grey and laid in thin layers by the water of old lakes, under the lakebeds and the chaos. Buff Mudstone shows on its scarps, and Dusted Mudstone, with red dust caught in its partings, in the upper layers of every exposure.
- Water Ice, the ice of the polar caps, layered with dust and hard as rock in their cold.
- Dry Ice, the carbon dioxide frost over the caps, and the seasonal ice over the spider fields.
- Silica Sinter, the opal old hot springs laid in pale layers, and Nodular Silica, opal in rounded knobs, in the terraces, mounds and chimneys of the Lifeless Springs; Silica Soil, white, under their red dust; and Digitate Silica, tufts of opal fingers on the nodules, as Spirit found them at Home Plate.
- Layered Sulfate, the light beds of the layered mounds.
- Fan Dust, the thin dark dust the spiders' jets lay over the ice.

Ores lie in veins in those rocks: hematite in the red regolith of the oxide plains and in the lakebeds' mudstone; magnetite and olivine in the basalt; and jarosite and gypsum, signs of old water, in the mudstone.

## How to get it

A crew reaches Mars by rocket. Count the launch down at [the launch panel](jsspace:launch_panel) and the rocket climbs from its pad to orbit. Mars goes round the Sun on an orbit of its own, so the transfer costs least in its window: the Flight Computer's XFER page shows how long until the next window opens, and what the burn costs now and in the window. The usual cruise takes {{jsspace:mars_trip}}. Spending more than the least shortens it, and a gravity assist spends less and takes longer. If everyone aboard sleeps, the cruise passes in their sleep. Solar storms, micrometeoroids and failures may come on the way. Arriving, a rocket with a heat shield can brake in Mars' air and save its capture burn; one without burns its engines.

From Mars' orbit, mark a spot on the LAND page and the rocket [comes down](jsspace:coming_down) somewhere in the landing ellipse round it. Mars has air, thin as it is, so the rocket needs a Heat Shield to come through it. The air is too thin to set it down, too: it needs legs, Landing Legs or a Lunar Lander Descent Stage, and propellant for {{jsspace:mars_landing_delta_v}}, the last burn down once the air has slowed it.

Every team knows where Mars is from the start. A server may also ask that a team knows what kills there before its crew lands: its atmosphere, temperature, radiation, gravity and relief.

The blocks of this page are found on Mars and mined there, each dropping itself: the regoliths, Basalt Sand, Silica Soil and Fan Dust with a shovel, the basalts, the mudstones, the silicas, Layered Sulfate and Water Ice with a pickaxe, Dry Ice with anything, and loose stones and Digitate Silica at a touch. They are also in the creative menu, J's Space tab.

To leave, set a transfer on the Flight Computer: when its time comes, the rocket lights its lowest stage left and climbs from the ground, with no pad.

### Landmarks

- The dichotomy, between the low smooth north and the old cratered south.
- Tharsis' volcanoes and the canyons of Valles Marineris, standing and cut where Mars' map of heights has them.
- In the sky, Phobos crossing it, and Deimos and the Overworld as points of light. At sunset the sky turns blue round the Sun.

### Biomes

- Northern Plains: the low smooth plains north of the dichotomy. Martian Regolith over Martian Basalt, the basalt bare on steep walls and its stones strewn about.
- Southern Highlands: the old high ground south of the dichotomy, crater on crater, the craters' rims of the basalt they broke. Regolith over basalt, and stones strewn about.
- Oxide Plains: stretches of the south where Hematite Regolith lies over the red, cratered and strewn with stones.
- Lakebed: stretches of the south where Mudstone lies bare, laid by old lakes, with the great craters that held them.
- Chaos Terrain: where the canyons open, ground broken into flat-topped blocks of mudstone under red regolith.
- Dark Dunes: crescents of dark Basalt Sand walking over the red ground, north and south.
- Polar Cap: Dry Ice frost over a thick layer of Water Ice, cut by spiral troughs.
- Lifeless Springs: old hot springs, dry for three billion years, at Gusev round Home Plate, at Nili Patera and in Vernal crater, and here and there in the south and the volcanic regions. Terraces of sinter step down, each pool held by a rim of nodules, among the low mounds of extinct geysers and short chimneys, Digitate Silica growing on the nodules.
- Layered Mounds: Gale's Mount Sharp and Valles Marineris' layered deposits. A mound of light sulfate and mudstone beds climbing in steps, buttes of mudstone under a dark cap, and yardangs the wind cut.
- Spider Fields: the cryptic region by the south cap, Inca City in it. Seasonal Dry Ice over the red ground, spiders carved through it by the gas escaping from under it, their troughs floored with dark sand, the dark fans of their jets, and the straight ridges of Inca City.

Mars' zones lie where they lie on the real planet, and inside each its biomes come and go every few thousand blocks: the south strewn with hematite here, an old lake there, dark dunes elsewhere.

## What can go wrong

> [!WARNING]
> Mars' air cannot be breathed: it is thin, almost all carbon dioxide, with no oxygen. Out of a sealed room you choke as under water, and the carbon dioxide gives you a headache. Outside, the tanks of [the Lunar Suit](jsspace:lunar_suit) worn whole are your only air.

> [!WARNING]
> Mars' cold, {{jsspace:mars_temperature}}, freezes an unprotected body. The Lunar Suit worn whole stands it.

> [!CAUTION]
> Flames need air, and Mars' holds no oxygen. Torches, lanterns, candles and campfires burn only in a sealed room with air, using up its oxygen, and furnaces burn their fuel only where a fire could. Light a base with lights that burn nothing, such as glowstone or a redstone lamp. J's Space's settings can let torches, lanterns, candles and campfires burn anywhere.

> [!CAUTION]
> Dry ice warmed, by air above freezing on one of its faces or a bright light beside it, turns to carbon dioxide a little at a time, even in a sealed room, until the block is gone. One block holds as much gas as hundreds of blocks of air: a warm habitat with dry ice in it fills with carbon dioxide, which gives a headache and then chokes.

> [!CAUTION]
> Water ice warmed the same way melts into water only in a sealed room with air. Anywhere else on Mars the air is too thin for water, and the ice turns straight to vapour and is gone.

> [!NOTE]
> Everything living there falls by Mars' pull, {{jsspace:mars_gravity}}: a jump carries you higher, and you come down slower.

> [!NOTE]
> Die on Mars and you come back at your bed there, if it stands in a sealed room with air; else in the cabin of the module you landed in, if it is still whole and has air; else at home, in the Overworld.

> [!PROBLEM] A slow dose of radiation builds up.
> The sky's radiation reaches the open ground, and Mars' thin air stops almost none of it. Every solid block overhead stops most of it, so live under a roof of rock or regolith; the Radiation Vest, fitted to the suit, stops part of it too.

> [!PROBLEM] The landing is held in orbit.
> The server asks that your team knows what kills on Mars first, and it does not yet: its atmosphere, temperature, radiation, gravity and relief. Or the rocket has no heat shield, or no legs or too little propellant, and the server holds such landings rather than let them burn up or crash.

> [!PROBLEM] The rocket burns as it enters the air.
> It has no heat shield. It loses blocks from its top, and its parachutes with them. Fit a Heat Shield before coming down.

> [!PROBLEM] The rocket crashes at touchdown.
> It had no legs, or too little propellant left for the last burn of {{jsspace:mars_landing_delta_v}}. Fit Landing Legs or a Lunar Lander Descent Stage, and keep that much in the tanks.
