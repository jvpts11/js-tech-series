# Coming down

## What it is

How a [capsule](jsspace:capsule) or a [lander](jsspace:lander) comes down from a world's orbit to its ground, flown by its [flight computer](jsspace:flight_computer). It lands somewhere in an ellipse round the spot marked: 15.4 by 13.2 km with the computer alone, half that with a guidance platform in its instrument ring.

## What it is for

Setting a crew down on [the Moon](jsspace:the_moon), on [Mars](jsspace:mars), or back home.

## How to get it

A rocket comes down once it rests in a world's orbit with a landing on that world set on the LAND page: a spot marked and SET pressed.

## How to use it

The computer flies it all, step by step as the cards say. In the thick air of the Overworld, the parachutes set a capsule down. In the thin air of Mars they slow it to 40 blocks over the ground, then its engines brake it down to its legs. With no air, as on the Moon, the engines brake it all the way. [Landing platforms](jsspace:landing_platform) take spent stages, not capsules.

## What can go wrong

> [!WARNING]
> With no heat shield, a rocket coming down through air burns up as it enters and loses its top. The shield is the capsule's, and counts only while the capsule has lost no block.

> [!WARNING]
> With no parachutes, or no legs and propellant to brake on, it crashes and loses its lowest blocks in the blast.

> [!PROBLEM] Landing held: the crew may not land.
> Your team does not yet know the world's atmosphere, temperature, radiation, gravity and relief. Learn them, then set the landing again.

> [!PROBLEM] Landing held, and the rocket stays in orbit.
> The world's rules keep in orbit a rocket that would burn up or crash. Bring a heat shield, or legs and propellant, then set the landing again.
