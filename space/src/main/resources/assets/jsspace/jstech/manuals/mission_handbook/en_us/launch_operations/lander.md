# Lander and Landing Legs

## What it is

What a rocket stands on when it comes down. Landing Legs is a part one layer tall, three or five blocks across: a Landing Leg at each corner with Leg Actuators beside it, and on the large one a Leg Mount in the middle of each side. The Lunar Lander Descent Stage, five blocks across and three tall, has its Descent Engine under two layers of Descent Foil, a Lander Leg in the middle of each side and the Lander Ladder on one; the cabin, its ascent stage, stands on it. Both fold their legs on the way up and spread them to land.

## What it is for

Landing where the air is too thin for parachutes alone, as on [the Moon](jsspace:the_moon) and [Mars](jsspace:mars), with propellant left to brake. A spent stage with legs and an engine can come back to a [landing platform](jsspace:landing_platform). On the ground, the ascent stage lifts off and leaves the descent stage behind as its launch platform, as the Apollo lunar modules did.

## How to get it

From the creative menu, J's Space tab.

## How to use it

1. On the Rocket Design screen, put Landing Legs over a stage's engine, under its tanks; folded, they lie along the tank.
2. Stand the lander's descent stage on a five-block Instrument Ring at the top of the rocket, its ascent stage on that.
3. Set the landing on the [flight computer](jsspace:flight_computer), and keep propellant to brake.

## What can go wrong

> [!WARNING]
> A lander with no legs, or with no propellant left to slow down, crashes.

> [!PROBLEM] The landing will not begin.
> The rocket has no legs, or no propellant to brake on them, and the server has turned crash landings off: it stays in orbit.

> [!PROBLEM] The screen says a part does not fit.
> Its foot is not as wide as the top of the part under it. The lander is five blocks across: build the rocket under it at five.
