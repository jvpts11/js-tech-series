# Structures

What others left on the worlds: the ruins of the people who came before, the Enigmatic sites of an older civilization,
and the living cities of two kinds of beings. Structures show; they don't explain. Whatever story they tell is left for
the player to piece together, and on purpose it is not written here. The historic sites of real spaceflight are in
[Worlds](worlds.md).

## What exists today

Nothing of J's Space exists yet. The structure generation kit of J's Core that these are built on is designed with J's
Space and still to be built.

## To build

### Where they are, and how they are found

- **They generate as Minecraft's structures do**, as villages do, with the spacing and rarity of each kind set per
  world; worlds are endless, so they never run out. **Nothing generates within about 1,000 blocks of a world's origin**,
  where a player lands first: whoever wants a structure goes out looking.
- **Human ruins** lie on almost every world, rarer on the distant ones. **Enigmatic sites** lie on Mars and Eurynome in
  the Solar System, and on many worlds of other stars. **Cities** stand on Fulgor and Cindara II.
- **Finding them** is a property of the world ([Exploration](exploration.md)). From orbit, a satellite or probe with the
  **archaeological instrument** marks structures on the Atlas, imprecisely; a rover, or a player with the instrument in
  hand, narrows the spot; and the Space Research Computer, processing archaeological data, reveals others.
- They are built on **J's Core's structure generation kit**: placement rules per world, foundations that fit the ground,
  layouts of many pieces, settlements that grow over time, weathered variants made by rule, loot that only adds another
  mod's items when that mod is installed, structures instruments and maps can find, and state kept for each structure.

### Human ruins

What is left of the people who came before:

| Ruin | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| **Spaceport** | a pad and a hangar in ruins, with **a half-assembled rocket**: damaged parts to salvage; **data of old missions**, which give discovery of other worlds |
| **Abandoned city** | buildings, streets and squares; with J's Computers installed, **dead networks**, with hardware to salvage and **pre-trained AI models** on old media (through J's Computers' loot function); with J's Industrial installed, **rusted machines** |
| **Pyramid** | a **sealed chamber**; inscriptions in a worn human language that research deciphers; **a unique artefact** in each one |
| **Laboratory** | half buried: **their research on the Enigmatic sites**, the coordinates of sites they found, broken instruments |
| **Bunker** | fully buried, with a hidden entrance: **untouched supplies**, items as good as new, and the most fragmented records, from the end |
| **Mining installation** | equipment to salvage, and **ore already processed**, stored before it was abandoned |
| **The farthest outpost**, only on Terminus | the **unfinished starship** in its dock: parts and data for whoever reaches the edge ([The universe](universe.md)) |

- **Waking a ruin**: a **Portable Power Pack** plugged into the ruin's power panel brings its **terminals** back to
  life.
- **Studying it**: terminals and inscriptions give **archaeological data**, which the Space Research Computer turns into
  the team's knowledge, and that knowledge opens the **sealed chambers** through J's Core's knowledge gate.
- **What can be taken**: damaged rocket and starship parts, which can be repaired; **RTGs**, J's Space's power far from
  the Sun on its own ([Energy](energy.md)); discovery data; coordinates of Enigmatic sites; with J's Computers
  installed, hardware and AI models.
- **The look**: blocks of J's Space and of the mods installed, in a **weathered** version, as vanilla's copper oxidises:
  rusted metal, dead panels, cracked glass.

### Enigmatic sites

The buildings of an older civilization. They have no terminals and no explanation: **they are there to explore and to
measure**, and their data feeds the team's **Enigmatic comprehension**
([Enigmatic technology](enigmatic-technology.md)). They all speak **the same visual language**, and whoever pays
attention sees the pattern repeat from one site to another, and in the humanoids of the cities.

| Site | Size | What it is like | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Monolith** | small, 3 to 7 blocks tall | a single piece that **pulses light in a mathematical sequence**: the **prime numbers**, the same idea as the signal of the film *Contact* | its data, processed, **reveals the next monolith**; they form a **network** that leads to the larger sites |
| **Impossible Temple** | large, 15 to 30 blocks | **bigger inside than outside**, with corridors that come back to where they started: the non-Euclidean space of J's Core's physics kit | at its centre, the **crystal** holding **knowledge fragments**; it reveals **a star map** of temples on other worlds |
| **Cosmic Circle** | medium, a ring 15 to 25 blocks wide | its stones **bend light** around them, like a gravitational lens, in an effect on the image | it points to a site on another world of the same system, or of a neighbouring one |
| **Doorless Tower** | 10 to 20 blocks tall | solid, with no entrance | it **opens** when a player **repeats on it the sequence** of the nearest monolith, in pulses: a puzzle; inside, a chamber with **a unique artefact** |
| **Underground Cathedral** | huge, buried | a chamber of impossible geometry | the greatest gathering of knowledge fragments on its world |

**The site on Mars lies at Cydonia**, where Viking 1 photographed the **"Face on Mars"** in 1976: a hill that, in low
light, looked like a face. In reality it was only a hill; in the game, someone left something there.

**The "Wow!" signal.** In 1977, the Big Ear radio telescope caught a signal 72 seconds long from Sagittarius, so strong
that the astronomer Jerry Ehman wrote **"Wow!"** on the printout. It never came again. In the game, a large radio
telescope pointed at Sagittarius can **catch a signal** that leads to an Enigmatic site in another system.

### Cities

The two living civilizations of the game: the **electron humanoids** of Fulgor and the **magnetic humanoids** of Cindara
II ([Worlds](worlds.md)).

- **Cities** are born with the world, through J's Core's structure kit, from **villages** of 5 to 10 buildings to
  **cities** of 20 to 40, their final size set when they generate. They **grow slowly** while players are on the world,
  up to that size, so whoever comes back finds a larger city.

| | Electron humanoids (Fulgor) | Magnetic humanoids (Cindara II) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The look** | ethereal: platforms held up by arches of plasma; everything pulses with light | impossible for gravity: magnetite at angles that couldn't stand |
| **The centre** | the **Plasma Tower**, 20 to 35 blocks tall, with the **Current Core** (a block that can't be taken), pulsing in a mathematical rhythm | the **Polarity Tower**, tilted at an impossible angle, with the **Polarity Core** |
| **The rest** | floating platforms; arches; translucent dwellings; a square; guard posts; the fragment depot | domain walls; hovering pillars; crooked dwellings; the alignment square; guard posts; the fragment chamber |
| **The blocks** | their own, never crafted: solidified plasma, panels, pillars | structural magnetite, panels, domain pillars |

**The humanoids**, all with simple behaviour:

- **They react to the player's technology**: electron humanoids pulse faster near electric equipment, magnetic ones
  change the field around them. It is only visual.
- **The artefacts and fragments** of their cities give data for the team's **comprehension**, sometimes with the clue to
  an Enigmatic site.
- **They can be measured**: a multimeter reads the anomalous field of the electron humanoids, and a compass points at
  the magnetic ones.
- **They fall in step with the sites**: when a player disturbs an Enigmatic site, the humanoids nearby change together
  for a while, in a coordinated pulse.
- **Mourning**: when one dies, the others gather around the body for a while.
- **Guardians are neutral**: they only attack whoever attacks a humanoid or a guardian.
- **A city remembers aggression against a team**: attack one of its humanoids, and the whole city turns hostile to that
  team for good.
- **Guardians warn** whoever breaks the city near them, and **attack if they go on**.
