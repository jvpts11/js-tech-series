# Manuals

How a player learns J's Space in the game. The guides are a framework of J's Core: the series' manual, the Technical
Reference, has a chapter for every mod, and every mod has a manual of its own, in its own style, made from the same
entries written once.

## What exists today

J's Core's guide framework exists, with the Technical Reference and the manuals of J's Computers (the Guide to
Operations) and J's Industrial (Plant Drawings).

J's Space has its own manual, the Mission Handbook, the first thing in its creative tab. It is dressed as a crew's
flight documents were: an off-white ring binder with the mission's emblem and typed lines on the cover, cream pages
typed in capitals, a tab at the edge for each section in its colour and lettered with its code, and pages numbered by
section (LO 2-3). It has five sections: Launch Operations, Flight, Suits, World Data and Starships. It is written in
English and Brazilian Portuguese, and its chapter is in the Technical Reference too.

Every item of J's Space has its entry. Launch Operations holds the rocket hangar, the tanks, the engines, the capsule,
the lander, the mobile launcher, the crawler, the launch pad, the propellant depot and the launch panel. Flight holds
the flight computer, coming down, the landing platform and rocket wreckage. Suits holds the Lunar Suit, its modules and
its supplies. Starships holds the Small Shipyard, the hull, the engines, the wings and gear, the fittings, flying a
ship, the crew, the ship landing pad and the runway beacon. Each drill is on cuff cards, three steps a card.

World Data has a sheet for the Moon and one for Mars, each painted as seen from orbit, with its ground, its ores, how a
crew gets there and back, its landmarks, its biomes and what kills there. Its figures (diameter, gravity, air,
temperature, day and year, the usual trip and the delta-v to land) are read from the world's own data, so a data
pack's change shows on the page.

## To build

### The Mission Handbook

J's Space's manual is the **Mission Handbook**, in the style of NASA's real mission documents:

- **checklists** for launch, landing and spacewalks. Apollo's astronauts carried their checklists **on the cuff of their
  suits**, and the handbook looks like that little cuff book;
- **a sheet for every world**, as NASA's fact sheets: its conditions, its sky, its landmarks, what can be found there,
  and a pixel painting of the world seen from orbit;
- the **models** of rockets, starships and stations, turning, part by part, as J's Industrial's manual shows its
  machines;
- the hidden planets, Enigmatic sites and cities only appear once found: the handbook shows what the team knows.
  Recipes, as always, are all shown.

The same entries are J's Space's chapter of the **Technical Reference**. Holding the manual key over a block of J's
Space opens its entry.
