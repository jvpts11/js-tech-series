# Megastructures

The largest things a player builds: around stars, around worlds, and from the ground to orbit. Each belongs to a team.

## What exists today

Nothing of J's Space exists yet.

## To build

### The Megastructure Assembler

A huge factory, on the ground or on a station, that makes the **sections** of everything below.

### The Dyson swarm

The **precursor** of the Dyson Sphere: a swarm of solar collectors orbiting a star, built **collector by collector**,
each a section launched into the system's space. It gives energy **in proportion** to the collectors that exist and
sends it all by beam to **rectennas** ([Energy](energy.md)). It is **cheaper** than the sphere, but **its panels wear
out** over time and vanish, and have to be **replaced**.

- **The real idea**: Stuart Armstrong and Anders Sandberg worked out in 2013 how to **take Mercury apart** to build a
  swarm. In the game, a mine and a Mass Driver on Mercury feed the Assembler.
- **In the sky**, the star becomes **ringed with glints** and slowly dims. A star seen like that from far away was a
  real case: in 2015 "Tabby's star" dimmed so strangely that people spoke of a Dyson swarm (it was dust).
- It can be built around **any star** a team reaches; a red dwarf gives much less. Several teams can each have their
  swarm around the same star, which has light to spare.

### The Dyson Sphere

After the swarm, the real thing: **a shell around a star**, built section by section in the Megastructure Assembler, the
greatest source of energy in the game. A rigid shell around a star is the series' bet, and it doesn't wear out.

### The Space Elevator

**A tether from the ground to orbit.** A **climber** goes up it, with cargo and people, spending only **energy**, no
propellant, and passes into the system's space in the world's orbit, as a slow launch ([Travel](travel.md)).

- On the Earth, the tether is **carbon nanotubes or graphene**, the real candidates ([Minerals](minerals.md)).
- **On the Moon and on Mars it is far easier**: a lunar elevator could be built **with today's materials**, such as
  Kevlar. It is real, and so the lunar elevator comes long before the Earth's.

### The Orbital Ring

**A whole ring around a planet**, Paul Birch's idea (1982), held aloft by a rotor running inside it; built in sections,
in the system's space.

- It is a **mega-station**: room for racks, factories and homes, with no bonus of any kind.
- **Elevators** come down from it to the ground, and a **Mass Driver** built into it launches to other worlds.
- The number of its sections, and what fits in each, follow the real size of things, as J's Computers' racks of 2 by 3
  by 2 blocks.

### The Mass Driver

**An electromagnetic rail** of variable length: longer is faster and reaches further. Its magnets are of **cindarite**
and its superconductors of **onnesite**; the **unified field generator** makes the definitive one
([Enigmatic technology](enigmatic-technology.md)).

- **Cargo only, never people**: the acceleration would kill them.
- **The real idea**: in the 1970s Gerard O'Neill proposed **a Mass Driver on the Moon** launching lunar material into
  space, and built prototypes. On the Moon, with no air and little gravity, it works; on the Earth, air gets in the way,
  so **its place is the Moon and the airless worlds**.
- It needs a **catcher** at the destination, as the "mass catcher" O'Neill placed at the L2 point.

### Before them

**Solar power satellites** with their **rectenna** are the rung before, in the middle of the game ([Energy](energy.md)).
