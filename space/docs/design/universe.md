# The universe

Where J's Space takes the player: twenty-six star systems in three galaxies, the worlds in each, what each world is like
to stand on, how a world is made and what its sky shows. How the player gets there is in [Travel](travel.md); what each
world holds underground is in [Minerals](minerals.md); the life, the biomes and the landmarks of each world in detail
are in [Worlds](worlds.md).

## What exists today

Nothing of J's Space exists yet. J's Core already has what the worlds are built on:

- **dimension rules**, a datapack file per dimension with its gravity, whether its air can be breathed, its temperature,
  its weather and its pressure, worked out the same way by the server and by every player's game;
- the **hazard event** a dimension posts before its air, heat or cold harms a player, which a space suit cancels;
- **dimensions made while the game runs**, copies of a declared template saved with the world, which is how a star
  system's space appears the first time someone reaches it.

## To build

### What a player can visit

- **Every round world with a solid surface** can be visited: planets, dwarf planets and moons.
- **Small bodies that aren't round** are reached by probe only: landers and mining probes, never on foot. They are
  Phobos, Deimos, Psyche, Arrokoth and comet 67P, every one of them a body real probes visited or are on their way to.
- **Giant planets** (gas giants, ice giants and mini-Neptunes) have no surface: aeroprobes go down into them.
- **No world is water everywhere.** A world with a sea always has land, ice or a shore to walk on and for structures to
  stand on. A generator for worlds that are all ocean is in [the API](api.md) for add-ons; the series' own worlds don't
  use it.
- **Every star system has a space of its own**, a dimension made the first time someone gets there, where its worlds are
  spheres in their places, moons around their planets. **A world's orbit is the space around its sphere**: vacuum, no
  gravity, the world below filling half the view; stations, satellites and debris stay there. Distances are squeezed far
  more than sizes, as in No Man's Sky, so worlds look grand and journeys stay short enough to fly: about 10,000 blocks
  for an astronomical unit, the Solar System as far as Neptune about 300,000, and Oceanus about 5 million, within
  Minecraft's limit of 30 million ([Travel](travel.md)).
- **Asteroid belts** (the main belt and the second Kuiper belt beyond Terminus) are fields of small rocks floating in
  their system's space, for mining ([Mining in space](mining.md)).
- **Some places have no world at all**, only something to gather: the dead stars (the pulsar Lich, the white dwarf
  Sirius B), the radiation belts, and a **kilonova remnant** of the series' own in the Milky Way, the cloud left by two
  neutron stars that merged ([Minerals](minerals.md)).

Two things this table makes a player learn. **Jupiter's radiation is the hardest danger of the Solar System**: on Io, 36
sieverts a day kill in minutes without heavy shielding, while Callisto, outside Jupiter's belts, gets almost nothing,
which is why NASA studied Callisto for a human base near Jupiter. And **gravity can be almost nothing**: on Enceladus or
Mimas a jump lasts a long time.

### Sedna

Discovered in 2003, about 1,000 km wide and one of the reddest things in the Solar System. Its orbit takes about 11,400
years: today Sedna is about 84 astronomical units out and coming in to its closest point (76 AU, around 2076), and at
the far end it goes out to nearly 900 AU. **It spends most of its orbit outside the heliosphere**, the bubble of the
Sun's wind that ends at about 120 AU, in interstellar space; today it is on the inside.

Its red ground is tholin over ices of methane and ethane, where the James Webb Space Telescope found ethane, acetylene
and ethylene in 2023, chemistry made by cosmic rays in methane ice. The sky is black and the Sun a point of light, still
as bright as about sixty full moons. Sedna has no moon (Hubble looked in 2004), so its mass can't be measured and its
gravity is an estimate. **Sedna's orbit is the first clue of the hidden planets below.**

### The hidden planets

Two planets of the Solar System come from real predictions that nobody has confirmed yet. **They are not on the map at
the start.** A team finds them the way Neptune was found in 1846, when Urbain Le Verrier worked out where an unseen
planet had to be and Johann Galle found it the same night, less than a degree from the spot: the team observes Sedna and
the other distant bodies, gathers their orbits, its Space Research Computer works out where the unseen mass has to be,
and a telescope pointed there finds it ([Exploration](exploration.md)). Finding each one is a milestone.

The first team to find a hidden world names it, and its moons, for the whole server, the way a discoverer proposes a
name to the International Astronomical Union. A setting turns this off, and then every hidden world has its **default
name**: Greek and Roman gods, as the IAU names planets and their moons.

**Terminus** (Planet Ten). In 2017, Kathryn Volk and Renu Malhotra found that the average plane of the Kuiper Belt
between 50 and 80 AU is warped by about 8°, and that a body as massive as Mars at about 60 AU would explain it. Terminus
is that body: it clears the band between 50 and 70 AU, which is why the Kuiper Belt seems to stop at 50 AU, the "Kuiper
cliff", and the second belt lies beyond, from about 70 AU, where a survey with the Subaru telescope for New Horizons
found bodies in 2024. Terminus is named after the Roman god of boundary stones.

- A rocky world: an iron core, a silicate mantle and a crust of frozen gases. About 6,800 km, 0.38 g, a day of 21 hours,
  an orbit of about 465 years.
- **Volcanoes**, kept alive mostly by the heat of its radioactive rock, as Mars kept its volcanoes for most of its
  history; its moons' tides add a little.
- **Glaciers of nitrogen** that flow, with the convection cells of Pluto's Sputnik Planitia.
- **Seas of liquid oxygen** in the basins the volcanoes warm, at 55 to 60 K: oxygen melts at 54 K and boils at 90 K, so
  everywhere else it is frozen. Solid oxygen is denser than liquid and sinks, so the **icebergs are of nitrogen**, solid
  and lighter between 54 and 63 K. The oxygen is as old as the Solar System: the Rosetta probe found molecular oxygen
  trapped in comet 67P in 2015, and cosmic rays splitting water ice make more.
- A sea of oxygen beside methane and tholin is explosive: a spark on the shore can start a violent fire.
- Its rocks are rich in **rare earths**, with the thorium and uranium whose heat drives the volcanoes, as the KREEP
  rocks of the Moon bring potassium, rare earths and phosphorus together with thorium and uranium.
- Two moons, **Cardea** (about 1,100 km) and **Limentinus** (about 600 km), round but light: together under 0.5% of
  Terminus' mass, where a double planet like Pluto and Charon would need about 10%. They are Roman gods of the doorway:
  Terminus the boundary, Cardea the hinge, Limentinus the threshold. Terminus' tides give Limentinus **geysers**.

**Oceanus** (Planet Nine). In 2016, Konstantin Batygin and Mike Brown showed that the orbits of the most distant bodies,
Sedna among them, cluster in a way only an unseen planet explains: about five times the Earth's mass, 400 to 500 AU out,
tilted about 20°. Oceanus is that planet, named after the Titan of the river that circles the whole world.

- A mini-Neptune: a core of rock and ice inside an envelope of hydrogen and helium with methane, pale blue-grey, about
  40 K from its own inner heat, an orbit of about 10,000 years. Reached by aeroprobe.
- The Sun shines there like two full moons: an endless twilight.
- Narrow, dark rings, like those of Uranus and Neptune.
- **Doris**, the moon of the ocean (about 2,000 km): its eccentric orbit lets Oceanus' tides warm it from inside, as
  Jupiter warms Io and Europa, so it keeps an **ocean of water and ammonia under the ice**, 500 AU from the Sun, with
  ice volcanoes and a very thin nitrogen air. Its ocean has chemistry and no life.
- **Acheron**, the dark moon (about 800 km): old, cratered, covered in black carbon-rich dust; the darkest place a
  player stands on.
- **Eurynome**, the captured moon (about 500 km): red with tholins, on a backwards orbit, as Triton, which was also
  captured. It came from the inner Oort Cloud and holds **the Solar System's second Enigmatic site**, after Mars.

### The gate to the stars

Everything the first journey to another star needs comes together at Terminus and Oceanus.

- **The map of the stars.** Navigating to a star needs its distance, and a star's distance is measured from two places
  far apart. In 2020, New Horizons, 47 AU out, photographed Proxima Centauri and Wolf 359 and measured the first stellar
  parallax ever taken from a probe. An astrometry observatory on Terminus, 60 AU from the Earth, measures the near stars
  precisely, and **those measurements open interstellar navigation** ([Exploration](exploration.md)).
- **Fuel.** Liquid oxygen from the seas of Terminus, methane from its ice, and **ice rich in deuterium**: bodies of the
  cold outer Solar System hold more of it, and comet 67P has three times the share of the Earth's water. Deuterium is
  the fusion fuel of starships.
- **Superconductors for free.** At 35 K, high-temperature superconductors such as YBCO, which lose all resistance under
  93 K, work without cooling, so huge magnets are cheap on Terminus.
- **Thorium and uranium** in its volcanic provinces.
- **A human ruin**, the farthest outpost of the people who came before, with an unfinished starship in its dock.
- **Cardea is the shipyard**: almost no gravity, next to Terminus' fuel, so leaving it costs almost nothing.
- **Limentinus' geysers** feed a ring of ice in orbit, as Enceladus feeds Saturn's E ring, and give ammonia and the
  chemistry of a hidden ocean.
- **Doris' ocean** can be drilled through the ice down to hydrothermal vents, for ammonia and deuterium.
- **Acheron is the best observatory of the Solar System**: as cold as the James Webb Space Telescope works, which needs
  a sunshield the size of a tennis court for it, and far from every radio noise of the Earth. Its black dust holds
  **presolar grains**, nanodiamonds and silicon carbide older than the Sun, as are found in the Murchison meteorite.
- **Oceanus is close to the Sun's own telescope.** From 550 AU on, the Sun's gravity bends the light of distant stars
  like a giant lens, and NASA studied a mission to use it and photograph the surface of a planet of another star. A
  **solar gravitational lens probe** launched from Oceanus gives the best discovery possible of a chosen planet of
  another star, before anyone goes there.

Worlds worth a word:

- **Tales.** In 2025 the James Webb Space Telescope found a giant planet candidate, about Saturn's mass, in the
  habitable zone of α Cen A. A world that orbits both stars of the pair wouldn't be stable there, but a moon of that
  giant would; a moon as large as Tales doesn't form around a giant, so Tales was captured.
- **Barnard's Star**, the second closest system, with four small planets confirmed in 2025, is the first step between
  the Sun and Alpha Centauri.
- **Lich's planets** were the first planets ever found outside the Solar System, in 1992, around a dead star. A
  supernova blows away whatever was around the star, so they were born afterwards, from a disc of debris.
  **Poltergeist** is the debris of a world the supernova destroyed, put back together: a planet-sized breccia of blocks
  of different rocks welded at crooked angles, with sharp borders between them, shock glass in the seams, slivers of the
  old iron and nickel core rich in platinum metals, and deep cracks where the pieces never fitted. **Phobetor** is a
  carbon planet. **Draugr**, twice the Moon's mass, may be a sliver left over.
- **Hera.** The centre of the galaxy has had generation after generation of stars enriching its gas, so it holds far
  more metals than our neighbourhood: Hera is a super-Earth full of heavy metals, iron, nickel, platinum and uranium. In
  the cluster of stars at the centre its night is never dark, with thousands of stars brighter than Venus. Its name is
  the goddess whose spilt milk, in the myth, made the Milky Way.
- **Archaeon and Nereus** carry complex life, which took the Earth four billion years, so they orbit old stars: 61
  Cygni, about six billion years old and the first star whose distance was measured, by Friedrich Bessel in 1838, and
  Epsilon Indi, three and a half to five billion years old, with a cold giant the James Webb Space Telescope
  photographed in 2024. Nereus' auroras and its useless compass come from its own magnetic field.
- **LHS 1140 b, the eyeball world.** A study of 2024 with data from the James Webb Space Telescope found it is most
  likely a world of ice with **a single liquid ocean**, a circle about 4,000 km across always facing its star, about 20
  °C at the centre. The whole planet is ice except for a blue eye under a red sun that never moves.
- **Specula** orbits Laelaps, the dead world of Sirius: when Sirius B was a red giant, it melted Specula's surface into
  natural glass. Laelaps is the hunting dog that never missed its prey, near Sirius, the Dog Star.
- **Nox** has no star to light it, only two brown dwarfs; Nox is the Roman goddess of night.

- **Verath C, the ringed world.** A rocky planet with a ring, like a Saturn of stone. A study of 2024 proposed that the
  Earth itself had a ring 466 million years ago: a large asteroid broke apart too close, and the rain of its pieces
  would explain why the craters of the Ordovician all lie near the old equator. On Verath C, the ring is a thin line at
  the equator and a giant orange arch in the light of the red giant further north or south, so the sky changes with
  where the player stands; the ring's shadow lies on a band of latitude, colder, a band of snow in the middle of warm
  lands (the same study links the shadow to the Earth's ice age of that time); meteors fall all the time from the ring;
  and in Verath C's orbit the ring is there to mine.
- **Carbon worlds are Ithel's.** Carbon stars are commonest where there are few metals, as in the Triangulum Galaxy.
  Adamas is Greek for diamond.
- **Vorna B** is the opposite of Hera: a world poor in iron, whose crust is almost pure olivine, green.

### Worlds that always face their star

A planet close to a red dwarf turns so that one face always looks at its star: one side always day, one always night,
the terminator between. It is real, and it is also tiring, so few worlds are like this: Proxima b and d, the six worlds
of TRAPPIST-1, LHS 1140 b (the eye only exists because the same face always looks at the star) and c, and the four
worlds of Barnard's Star. **The rest turn.** Two of them, 82 Eridani b and Gliese 667C c, turn three times every two
orbits, as Mercury does, so their sun still rises and sets, over a long day. Pluto and Eris always show the same face to
their moons, as measured.

### Every world is its own

A world is made of three layers:

- **a generator**, the process that shaped its ground;
- **modifiers**, laid over any generator;
- **its data**: everything that makes that world itself.

A generator is only a process: how wind carves, how craters pile up. **Two worlds of the same generator never look
alike**, because everything else is the world's own data:

- **look**: the blocks and palette of its ground, its rocks, the colour of its sky and of its sunset, its haze and its
  light;
- **physics**: gravity, atmosphere, temperature, day, radiation and the scale of its relief;
- **biomes of its own**: no world borrows a biome from another ([Worlds](worlds.md));
- **landmarks**: unique named landforms, each placed once in the world: on Mars, Olympus Mons, Valles Marineris, the
  Hellas basin, the polar caps with their spiral troughs, the craters Gale and Jezero;
- **code of its own**, through [the API](api.md), when data isn't enough.

Mars and Tau Ceti e share a generator. Mars has red iron-oxide regolith and dark basalt, high relief because 0.38 g lets
mountains rise, the dichotomy between its high cratered south and its low smooth north, a butterscotch sky with blue
sunsets, Phobos crossing the sky twice a day rising in the west, dust devils, dust storms over the whole planet and CO₂
snow at the poles. Tau Ceti e has pale sand of silicate poor in iron, low wide relief flattened by 1.6 g, no caps and
the sky of a thin nitrogen air.

| Physics | relief by gravity; gravity by latitude (a fast spin, as Haumea's); radiation fields (Jupiter's moons); **conditions by altitude** (Venus: crushing and burning at the ground, a breathable floor of air high in the clouds, where floating bases sit) |

**Relief follows gravity**, as in reality: Olympus Mons rises 22 km on Mars, while on the Earth nothing goes far above
10 km. Light worlds get tall relief (the ridge of Iapetus, the peak of Rheasilvia on Vesta, the cliff of Miranda) and
heavy worlds get low, wide relief. Everything is squeezed into the height of the world, which is the Overworld's, but
the proportion between worlds stays.

Generators, modifiers and landmarks are registered through [the API](api.md): an add-on adds its own, and a datapack
builds a new world out of the ones that exist.

### The size of a world

**A round world is an ordinary Minecraft world**, practically endless, as the Overworld. It doesn't wrap around: walking
east doesn't bring the player back from the west. A world's size is felt through its data, its gravity, its sky and its
day, not through an edge; on the real Moon the curve of the ground can't be seen either.

### The sky

**Real by the data, Minecraft by the look.** Each world's sky is worked out from real data: where its star and its
neighbours are, how big they look, their colour and the light of its day. It is drawn the way Minecraft draws its own
sky:

- **stars** are vanilla's small square points, in the places the data gives;
- **the sun** is square like vanilla's, with the size and colour of that star: a point at Sedna, red and filling about
  30° of the sky at Verath;
- **planets and moons** in the sky are drawn by hand in the style of vanilla's moon, in pixels, with their phases. From
  Io, Jupiter fills about 19° of the sky, nearly forty times the full Moon seen from the Earth; from the Moon, the Earth
  hangs still and shows phases; from Titan, Saturn and its rings; from Mars, Phobos rising in the west twice a day;
- the colour of the sky and of the haze is vanilla's in the tone of the place: black and starry at noon with no air,
  as in the Apollo photographs; butterscotch on Mars with blue sunsets; orange on Titan; yellow high in the clouds of
  Venus.

No photograph and no realistic sky: the look stays the game's.

**The planets are in the Overworld's sky too.** Each planet is a tiny texture in the sky, where it really is, moving
among the stars from night to night. To the eye it is a bright point, as in the real sky; through a telescope, its disc,
drawn by hand, **turning with the planet** at the Overworld's pace: Jupiter turns in about 8 real minutes, with the
Great Red Spot going by, Mars in about 20, Saturn in about 9. What Galileo saw in 1610 is there: **Jupiter's four
moons** as points that change sides from night to night, **the phases of Venus**, and **Saturn's rings** tilting over
the years until they stand edge-on and almost vanish, as they did in March 2025. Uranus and Neptune are tiny blue-green
discs, only in a large telescope. What a player sees depends on the telescope ([Exploration](exploration.md)). The
Overworld's sky is otherwise vanilla's.

### Day and night

**The Overworld keeps its day of twenty minutes**, so nothing that depends on it, in vanilla or in another mod, changes.
Every other world's day is in proportion to it: the Earth's 24 hours are the Overworld's twenty minutes, so Mars has
almost the same day. Long days are estimates, set by playing: the Moon's would give about five real hours of sunlight
and five of night, which turns into play (solar panels stop through the long lunar night, the real problem of the
Artemis bases), and Mercury's about 59 hours.
