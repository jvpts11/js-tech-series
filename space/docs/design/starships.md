# Starships

The second game of J's Space. Rockets open the Solar System; starships open the universe. A starship is built on the
ground from multiblock sections, flown by the player in the air and in space, lived in, and taken between the stars. How
starships travel is in [Travel](travel.md).

## What exists today

The small class is built: its shipyard, its parts, its modules, its flight, its landing places, its crew. Medium and
large ships, the interstellar drives, weapons and the rest of the sections are to build.

### The small class

A small ship is chosen for short trips, for specific jobs and for being easy to park; it is cheaper, easier and faster
to build than a large one. **Every starship is built of modules**, a spine of sections from the nose to the tail joined
by the class's one standard joint. Wings sit at the second section, nacelles on wings that carry them, the fins and the
tail engine at the spine's end, side intakes, thrusters round the hull and the gear under it. The hull has room for
**one medium module or two or three small ones**; there is no grid of slots, the ship's size limits it. A design flies
when it has a cockpit, an airlock, a reactor, a main engine, the gear and a whole spine, all its parts in their places
and of one class.

### Parts, makers and finishes

The small class has **25 parts in ten kinds**: the Orbiter, Cab and Greenhouse cockpits; the Airlock, the Docking Module
(its dorsal collar is an airlock too) and the Corridor; the Bay Doors, Ramp and Side Door holds; the reactor with flush
radiators or with radiator fins; the Twin Bells and the four-bell Cluster at the tail, and the Magnetic Nozzle in a
nacelle; the Side Intakes and the Nacelle Intake; the Blended Delta, the Stub Wings and the Swept Wings; the Twin
Canted Fins and the Single Fin; the Blister and Quad thrusters; Wheels and Legs.

Every part is a multiblock built of a **kit of 65 blocks**, the same for every maker. A part forms into its model when
its last block goes in, and its blocks take the model's shapes: a hull's floor and ceiling leave room to walk, a window
is glass the world is seen through, and the aim and the collision follow the model, not boxes.

**Makers.** Each maker owns a family of the catalogue, gives its parts their shape and, to every part a player goes into,
its interior. A ship may mix makers, changing style from module to module. **Meridian Orbital** (the orbiter line) has
padded white panels with a red line at hand height; **Ironside Yards** (the working line) has bare plates on ribs over
grating floors; **Kosmozavod** has turquoise panels over beige quilted padding on a wooden floor. The pilot's seat of each
is richly detailed, and the cockpits are alive: displays follow the ship, needles move, lamps light by state, the side
stick leans with the pilot's hand and the throttle slides forward.

**Finishes.** The paint is chosen apart from the parts, for the whole ship: the **Orbiter** (white blankets over black
heat tiles with a red band), **Bare Metal** (patched plates with a crimson band and hazard stripes) and **Enamel**
(crimson over cream with a gold line).

### Modules

A module takes a third of a small hull's room, a medium one the whole of it:

- the **Workshop**: a crafting table, a smithing table, a furnace and a barrel, which work aboard;
- the **Laboratory**: a bench, sample racks, a microscope and an analyser;
- the **Quarters**: a bunk that is a bed, a locker, a fold-down table;
- **Computing**: a desk and a server rack, joined to the network by the ship's antenna with J's Computers installed;
- **Small Cargo**: racks of barrels;
- the holds, which are medium modules: Bay Doors, Ramp and Side Door, with racks, tie-downs, a hoist rail and a manifest
  terminal, and barrels along both walls, one over another every second row.

The furniture stands in the cells of the walls, leaving a corridor two blocks high from module to module on one floor.
The Workshop's tables, furnace and barrel and the holds' barrels are blocks of the kit, the **Ship Crafting Table**,
**Ship Smithing Table**, **Ship Furnace** and **Ship Barrel**: each works as the game's own and is drawn by its
module's model, so a ship never carries a block of the game standing in for its fittings.

The airlock has an Oxygen Panel that fills a worn suit's tanks from the cabin's air and a Suit Locker with nine places
for a crew's suits and bottles. The reactor room has its fusion drum.

### The cabin and its air

The small ship's cabin is pressurized. Every block of the kit holds a crew's air against the vacuum, the furniture
standing in the cabin lets its air round it, and the helmet comes off inside. With no life support section, the flight
console keeps a **small air reserve**, two hours of one crew member's oxygen as a first estimate. It tops the cabin back
up to the sea's air as the crew breathe and the hull leaks, and an Oxygen Bottle used on the console pours into it. The
cabin is a sealed room wherever the ship flies, in space too, and keeps its air when the ship changes dimension. A
bunk is a respawn point only while its cabin has air.

### Building

The **Small Shipyard** is a dry dock: a pit sixteen blocks wide and twenty-four long, dug three deep, with a rim of
hazard concrete and a cradle of clamps rising from its floor; four lattice towers down each side, eleven blocks to the
rails of a gantry crane, with walkways at two heights and a stair; floodlights at the corners; at the back a control
booth, and at its foot an **Item Port**. The pit must be clear. The booth is entered by its door: the **Shipyard
Terminal** is the first half of its console under the windows on the bay, the parts list on the other half, and behind
them stand a bench a computer can stand on and a socket in the wall beside it, for the day the yard is worked from a
computer (below).

The terminal opens the **Starship Design**, in the look of a shipbuilder's screen: the ship on a dotted floor in a dark
void, turned and zoomed freely, with panels of dark glass, its words in the game's own letters. A part is dragged from
its card as a hologram of itself under the mouse, green where it would snap to a joint, red where it does not fit,
saying why; only the joints it fits show their green nodes. The part under the mouse is outlined by its own silhouette.
The screen shows the parts by kind, searched by name or maker, the chosen part's length, mass, blocks, power and note,
the flight check with the room left in the hull, the ship's mass, thrust, vertical take-off thrust against weight,
power and crew, the blocks the dock needs and the Item Port holds, and the footprint on the floor; F changes the
finish. A design is saved by name among the dock's plans, and sent to the dock once it flies and fits the bay.

A design sent to the dock appears as a **hologram**: every block still to place shows where it goes, the layer being
built glowing amber, and each part still to form has its box with a tag lifted over it, with its name, its size and how
many of its blocks are in place. The ship is built by hand on the hologram, or by the dock's **four robot arms**, which
take blocks from the feeders on the walkways, swing them to their place and weld them with sparks. What the ship needs
goes in through the Item Port, and no more: a player puts it in, or a hopper, a pipe of any mod or J's Computers'
network does. The dock has no conveyor of its own; conveyors come to the series with J's Industrial, through J's
Core.

The Shipyard Terminal and the Rocket Hangar's terminal are **machines, not computers**: they are not members of a data
network and take no data cable, and they work through their own screens. With J's Computers installed, when J's Space
grows, the yard's booth and the hangar's control room get **a place for a computer**, where an Aerospace Design
Computer runs AeroCAD and the other programs of the building, and J's Core's **network port** in the wall, which joins
that computer to the network; both are optional places of the structure ([J's Computers](computers.md)).

A ship built whole leaves the dock when its pilot uses its flight console: it becomes one moving structure, once, and
stays one, standing still or flying. A ship is changed only in a dock: its pilot brings it into a free dock's bay and uses
its console, and the dock takes it back onto the grid to change by the arms or by hand and let go again. A repair can be
made anywhere, by putting the right block back.

### A starship really flies

A starship's blocks are **real blocks that really fly**, on J's Core's **kit of moving structures**: machines run and
players walk inside the ship in flight, and what works on the ground works aboard. The ship rests on the land under it,
steps up what is low, and is stopped by the land it would fly into or that is not loaded yet. J's Transport uses the same
kit for its vehicles.

### Flying

- **The player flies a starship in the spirit of No Man's Sky**: it goes where its nose points, with no drift and no
  orbital sums. The pilot sits in the cockpit's pilot seat, which is the helm, opens and closes the throttle (it stays
  where it is left), turns, and pitches the nose up and down. In the air its air engines push, and in the thin air of the
  climb and in space its bells push. Its wings hold it up, more the faster it goes and less in thinner air, and flying too
  slowly it sinks. In space it flies free and faster. **Weight counts**: a heavier ship gathers speed more slowly and needs
  more of it to stay up; a ship too heavy for its engines only sinks.
- **Boost.** On its own key, the boost takes the ship faster across an empty stretch, well clear of the land or in space,
  from a store the reactor's spare power fills.
- **Taking off and landing.** The air engines' nozzles turn down, as the Harrier's do, just far enough to hold what the
  wings do not. Below flying speed the ship hovers with its nose level and the pilot takes it up and down. **Every small
  ship takes off and lands vertically**; the gear decides the rest. **Wheels** roll on the ground, on a runway, and also
  land on a pad. **Feet** stand where they come down, on rough ground. The gear is down on the land, when hovering and
  near the ground, and folds flat into the floor as the ship climbs away, struts first and bay doors shutting after,
  in about two seconds. With nobody at the helm a ship comes down gently and settles; its throttle closes, and it stays
  parked.
- **The climb.** A ship climbs to orbit without stopping, with the same passage into space as a rocket
  ([Travel](travel.md)): over the build limit it flies on through the open sky, and over the line it crosses into the
  system's space whole, with no loading screen, its pilot still at the helm.
- **The HUD.** Projected on the canopy, green, flat and in pixels: the heading tape, the pitch ladder with the flight path
  marker (which stands for the crosshair), the speed with the boost's bar, the altitude and climb, the reactor's power and
  deuterium, the nozzles' angle and the gear, and the autopilot's three modes. From behind, the camera stands back round
  the ship and the HUD spreads over the screen.

### Autopilots

- **LAND** flies the ship to the nearest landing pad its pilot may use, within about 512 blocks, high enough on its way,
  slowing as it nears, and sets it down hovering over the deck's middle. A ship on wheels may instead land on a runway
  marked by a Runway Beacon.
- **ORBIT** opens the throttle, pitches the nose up to a 55-degree climb and boosts, up and over the line into the system's
  space.
- **DOCK**, in space, turns the ship to face the nearest ship with a docking collar that would let the pilot aboard, at
  least as a passenger, within about 200 blocks, collar to collar, and brings it in on its thrusters until the two collars touch, in line.

The pilot taking the helm, holding any of its keys, lets the autopilot go. The HUD lights the mode engaged and draws its
symbol where its target stands in the world.

### Landing pads and runways

- The **Ship Landing Pad** is a multiblock where a starship sets down at a base. It has two builds: an octagon of Ship Pad
  Plates twenty-one blocks across, and a round deck of the same width raised three blocks on a truss, with a stair down one
  side and four light masts at its corners. Formed, it is painted with its touchdown ring, hazard band and cross, and lit
  round its edge. Whoever places its terminal owns it, and the owner can open it to their team or to everyone.
- The **Runway Beacon** is put down at the start of any flat stretch, facing the way it runs. LAND takes the runway when
  the stretch is long and clear enough: sixty-four blocks of firm level land, thirteen wide, six of headroom and no other
  ship on it. A ship on wheels flies to a gate a hundred metres back, down a six-degree glide, flares low and rolls on
  until it stops. The pilot is told when a stretch is not clear. These measures are first estimates.

### Docking

Docking is collar to collar. Once DOCK brings a ship's collar to touch another's, in line, the two are made one: the ship
docking is held by the other where it stands and goes wherever it goes. Whoever is inside either uses the collar over
their head and goes through it, neither cabin's air let out. The autopilot key lets the docking ship go.

### Crew and ownership

- A ship started from its dock belongs to its pilot. The owner is always a pilot, and alone sets the roles. The owner's
  team is the crew, and everyone else stays outside until named. A player who joins or leaves the owner's team gains or
  loses the team's role at once, through J's Core's teams.
- There are three roles, each holding the ones under it. A **passenger** comes aboard through the airlock, sits, sleeps and
  passes the docking collar. A **crew member** works the fittings and the machines aboard. A **pilot** flies the ship, puts
  in and breaks its blocks, and has a dock take it back.
- The roles are set on the crew page of the flight console, drawn as the flight computer's display: the owner, the owner's
  team, everyone else, and each player named (sixty-four at most, as a first estimate).

### Damage

A block of a formed part broken aboard or in a dock leaves a **hole**: the part stays formed and holds round it, the hole
is drawn as that block of its model gone, and the dock's hologram shows it. A holed hull lets its room's air out. A holed part
does only the share of its blocks still in it: a holed engine pushes less, a holed wing holds less up, a holed
reactor makes less power. Putting the right block back mends it. A rocket in flight follows the same rule.

## To build

### Three classes

| Class | Like | Tier | Reach between the stars | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Small** (built) | a fighter | T5 | about 10 light-years | |
| **Medium** | the Millennium Falcon | T6 | the whole Milky Way | one or two small ships, with an auxiliary ship bay |
| **Large** | several large multiblocks joined together | T7, with its intergalactic drive at T8 | other galaxies | small and medium ships |

A small ship is almost all cockpit, wings and engine; its journeys are short. A large ship is lived in. Starships have
**a style of their own**, the series', since no real starship exists to copy.

### Building

Each class is built in **its own shipyard**: the medium a large hangar, the large an open-air dock, huge. Only the small
shipyard is built. Building works as for rockets ([Rockets](rockets.md)); the missing piece is that, with J's Computers
installed, the Aerospace Design Computer's **AeroCAD** designs starships too and sends the design to the shipyard. Paint
is chosen from three finishes; painting freely is still to build.

### Flying, still to build

- **Between the stars**, the interstellar drive, engaged only from orbit ([Travel](travel.md)). **Within a system,** free
  flight with the boost across the empty stretches is built.
- **Weight on other worlds**: a large ship may be unable to take off from LHS 1140 b, at 1.9 g.
- **Docking** at a station's ports and into a carrier's bay, flying into the large ship's hangar. Collar to collar between
  ships is built.

### Interstellar drives

| Drive | Class | The physics behind it | Fuel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Short** | small | **fusion** and **antimatter**, below the speed of light, with time squeezed | deuterium and helium-3; antimatter cells |
| **Galactic** | medium | **warp**: the space around the ship moves (Miguel Alcubierre's metric, 1994) | **negative-mass exotic matter**, and the reactor |
| **Intergalactic** | large | **the larger warp**, a bubble only a large hull can hold | far more negative-mass exotic matter |

**Negative-mass exotic matter** exists in no nature and no real physics. J's Space makes it in **its own Casimir
generator**, simpler and slower, starting from the Casimir effect (negative energy between two plates, measured in
1997); with J's Industrial installed, its Casimir Array is the scale path.

### Weapons and shields

Weapons are J's Space's own, **always fired by a player at the controls**:

| Weapon | What it is | The real thing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cannon** | shells in bursts | real rotary cannons |
| **Laser** | a beam of energy; it also mines | real defence lasers, such as the US Navy's HELIOS |
| **Missiles** | guided onto a locked target | |
| **Energy shield** | holds off blows until it is spent, and recharges | the series' bet |

Targets are other players' ships, when the server allows PvP, and the hostile creatures of the worlds
([Worlds](worlds.md)). With J's Warfare installed, it adds its own weapons through J's Space's API.

### Damage, still to build

**Every multiblock of a starship has durability.** When it reaches zero, **the multiblock breaks somewhere and some of
its blocks are really destroyed**: a ship back from a fight shows its holes. A destroyed engine takes away thrust, a lost
wing takes away lift, and a holed hull decompresses its room (J's Core's sealed-room kit). A missing block already costs its part
its share of the part's figures. A repair kit mends it. A destroyed ship becomes a **wreck**, part of which can be salvaged.

### What a starship spends

The reactor burns **deuterium** as the ship flies, a full load lasting about four hours aloft. With none left, the reactor
makes no power and the boost fills no more; a dock fills the load again. The boost lasts about eight seconds from a store
the reactor's spare power fills. Both are first estimates.

Still to build: the main engines spending propellant, fusion fuel on the larger ships (deuterium and helium-3 for the
short drive), the weapons spending the reactor's energy, and a gas scoop that refuels at a giant.

### Crew and ownership, still to build

- **There is no fixed crew limit**: a starship takes as many as it has seats, beds and hibernation pods for; its class
  already limits it.
- **A parked starship**, in a system's space or landed on a world, stays there when everyone leaves. What runs aboard
  follows the usual rules of loaded chunks; J's Core's Space Persistor keeps a ship loaded for whoever wants it to work
  on its own.
