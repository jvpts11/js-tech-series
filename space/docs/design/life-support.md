# Life support

What keeps a player alive away from the Earth: space suits and their modules, pressurized rooms, and the dangers of
every world. The rule is simple: **a suit's only clock is the air in its tanks**. The right protection gives the
ability to stay somewhere for as long as the player likes; no module slows the clock, and the only way to more time is
more air.

## What exists today

### The lunar suit

The lunar suit is a pressure suit in four pieces, after Apollo's A7L: the **Lunar Helmet**, hard and white with its
smoked visor in its frame; the **Lunar Suit**, with the life-support pack on its back (its hatch, vent, lamps, purge
unit, antenna and bottles); the **Lunar Trousers**; and the **Lunar Boots**. It seals only when all four are worn. Worn
whole it stands cold down to -160 °C and heat up to 125 °C, and protects like leather armour.

Its tanks hold half an hour of air. The air left is kept on the Lunar Suit, the piece with the tanks.

### Breathing from the tanks

Worn whole where the air round the player cannot be breathed, the suit gives the pure oxygen of its tanks at about a
quarter of an atmosphere (3.7 psi, what Apollo's suits held), and takes nothing from the room. Each second of breathing
spends a second of air. Where the air can be breathed, the player breathes it, the tanks are spared, and the helmet can
come off in a room with air. With the tanks empty the suit gives no more air, and the wearer breathes what is round.

**A hole** leaks the tanks four times faster and thins the suit by about a third of its pressure. Two holes leave too
little oxygen to breathe. A **Suit Patch** mends one hole.

### The suit's controls

While the Lunar Suit is worn, the suit's own display and control module shows on the HUD, drawn like the hazard
devices and stacked with the dosimeter: the suit's white case with its red and blue hose stubs, and a black face with
the suit's pressure on a needle from 0 to 5 psi, the time of air left, the state (sealed, oxygen low, holed and leaking,
or open), the oxygen and carbon dioxide on bars, four lamps (O2, PSI, CO2, FAN), and the purge, fan and light buttons.
With little oxygen left the screen's rim turns amber; holed, it turns red. One key holds the controls up close, twice the
size, and puts the hand and the rest of the HUD away until they are put down.

### Suit modules

A module is fitted by right-clicking it onto the piece it goes on, and a piece takes one of each kind. It comes back off the way a bundle gives back what it holds: the piece right-clicked with nothing in the hand gives its last fitted module back. Taking the Extra Oxygen off leaves the tanks no more air than they hold without it.

| Module | Fits | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Extra Oxygen** | Lunar Suit | the tanks hold half as much air again |
| **Radiation Vest** | Lunar Suit | stops half of the radiation the suit's wearer would take |
| **Suit Lamps** | Lunar Helmet | lights the way ahead, as a torch does |
| **Gold Visor** | Lunar Helmet | a gold-coated visor lowered over the helmet's own; it cuts all of the Sun's glare, and the view through it is a little gold |

These are first estimates.

### Supplies

- The **Oxygen Bottle** holds half an hour of breathing. It fills from the air of a sealed room, taking the oxygen out of
  it, or from any gas tank or line of oxygen, and it pours into a suit's tanks from the hand or clicked onto the suit.
- The **Suit Patch** mends one hole in a suit. Used on a rocket's hull where a micrometeoroid made a hole, it patches the
  hull too.
- In a starship, the Oxygen Panel fills the tanks of a worn suit from the cabin's air ([Starships](starships.md)).

### Sealed rooms on other worlds

On the Moon, on Mars and in space, the game's glass, glass panes, doors, trapdoors and fence gates do not hold a room's
air against the thin air or the vacuum outside, and they burst as the room is filled. Whole blocks of stone and rock
hold it. Torches, lanterns, candles and campfires go out where the air holds too little oxygen to burn: a candle or a
campfire is left unlit, and a torch or a lantern falls as an item with a puff of smoke. A consequence setting, "Flames
need air", lets them burn anywhere.

The rest of a room's life is J's Core's sealed-room kit: whoever breathes takes oxygen from the room and gives it carbon
dioxide, plants slowly turn it back, a high share of oxygen turns any flame into an inferno, oxygen at high pressure
poisons slowly, and air rushing out through a hole pulls items and players towards it (a setting turns the pull off).
The **Atmosphere Detector**, J's Core's, shows a room's pressure and composition.

### Radiation on the Moon and Mars

The sky's radiation reaches the open ground of the Moon at about 1.4 millisieverts a day of the game and of Mars at
about 0.7, a dose that builds up slowly in whoever stays long. The Radiation Vest stops half of it. Between worlds, a
solar storm is told to a ship's crew twenty seconds ahead and fills the open space with radiation for half a minute;
blocks between a body and the Sun shield it.

### Coming back after a death on another world

A player who dies on another world comes back in this order:

1. at their bed there, if it stands in a sealed room with air; a ship's bunk counts, as long as its cabin has air;
2. otherwise in the cabin of the module they landed in, if it is still whole and has air; the first landing marks it,
   a few blocks from the lander;
3. otherwise at home, in the Overworld.

On an airless world, a player reborn outside without a suit would die again at once.

### The dangers of Moon and Mars

| Danger | Where | Without protection | What protects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vacuum and thin air** | the Moon, Mars | suffocation when the air runs out | the lunar suit; a sealed room |
| **Heat and cold** | the Moon's noon and night, Mars' cold | burns or freezes | a suit whose range holds the temperature; a sealed room |
| **Poisonous air** | the carbon dioxide of Mars | poisons | the lunar suit |
| **Cosmic radiation** | the Moon, Mars, space | a dose that builds up slowly | the Radiation Vest |
| **Glare of the Sun** | the Moon and space, where no air softens it | the view whites out near the Sun; it does no harm | the Gold Visor |

## To build

### Space suits

Suits go **by condition, not along a single ladder**, because every world asks for something else. Titan, for one, is
the only place in the Solar System where a person could walk without a pressure suit: its air is at 1.45 atm, so warm
clothes and an oxygen mask are enough. The lunar suit above is the third of the pressure suits.

**Pressure suits**, for vacuum and thin air:

| Suit | Tier | What it is like | The real thing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The first suit** | T2 | stiff, for short spacewalks on a tether | Leonov's Berkut, 1965: on the first spacewalk his suit ballooned, and he had to let air out to get back through the hatch |
| **The modern suit** | T4 | more mobility; entered **through the back** | the American EMU and the Russian Orlan |
| **The new generation** | T5 | stands the cold of the polar craters (-240 °C) and walks better | the Artemis suits |
| **The counterpressure suit** | T6 | skin-tight: the pressure comes from the elastic fabric, not from air; light, the best mobility, and **a hole doesn't empty the whole suit** | MIT's BioSuit, Dava Newman's |
| **The Integral Suit** | T7 | pressure, heat, cold, radiation and strong fields in one | the series' bet |

**Other suits:**

- the **thermal suit with a mask**, for thick, cold air (Titan) and for the clouds of Venus, with protection against
  acid; it needs no pressure;
- the **hard suit**, for Venus (92 atm, 464 °C), Solum B (90 atm) and Fulgor: armour like the deep-sea diving suits (JIM
  in the 1970s, the Newtsuit), with active cooling. The Venera 13 lander lasted 127 minutes on Venus.

**What holes a suit.** The leak, the loss of pressure and the patch are built, but nothing in the game makes a hole yet.
A micrometeoroid is meant to be able to hole a suit.

### More suit modules

| Module | Protects from |
| --- | --- |
| **Heavy shielding** | radiation belts (Io, Europa) and violent stars (the pulsar Lich, the flares of Proxima and Cindara, the X-rays of Cygnus X-1) |
| **Anti-field module** | strong fields (Cindara II, Fulgor) |
| **Anti-dust module** | dust clinging to the suit, so none comes indoors |
| **Relativistic module** | gravity: it brings the gravity a player feels down to the Overworld's, or less, on any world |
| **Jetpack** | nothing: it moves the player freely in space, turning on every axis ([Travel](travel.md)), as the MMU Bruce McCandless flew untethered from the Space Shuttle in 1984 |

The Radiation Vest also stops the radiation of solar storms; what it does against the belts is left to heavy shielding.

### Exo-suits

With J's Industrial installed, a **space module** lets its exo-suits be worn in space. It only fits the **closed** ones,
the Advanced Exo-Suit (T5) and the Transcendent Exo-Suit (T7); the Hydraulic Exoframe and the Industrial Exo-Suit are
open frames and can't be sealed. With the space module, **those two exo-suits go anywhere**. In the Transcendent, life
support runs in a closed loop.

### Pressurized rooms

A player builds a home anywhere, on Mars or on the Moon, seals it and fills it with air, and inside takes off the suit.
The mechanics are J's Core's sealed-room kit; the blocks are J's Space's.

**What seals:** full blocks seal. **Vanilla glass and doors leak**: they don't hold the difference in pressure.
**Pressurized windows**, of several panes, as the windows of the International Space Station's Cupola, and **pressurized
doors** seal. The kit finds the closed volume; a room with a hole holds no air.

**Where the air comes from:**

| | What it is | The price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Air**, nitrogen and oxygen | 1 atm, as on the Earth and the International Space Station | **dearer**: nitrogen comes from the Atmosphere Extractor, and Mars has little (2.8% of its air) |
| **Pure oxygen** | at **a third of an atmosphere**, as in Apollo and Gemini | **cheaper**, but **fire turns into an inferno**: any flame, lava or spark from a machine spreads at once, as in Apollo 1. Pure, at 1 atm, it also poisons over time |

**The blocks:**

- the **Vent** releases the gas that comes through a gas pipe into the room it stands in;
- the **Oxygen Generator** is the Electrolyser ([Propellants](propellants.md));
- the **Air Mixer** blends nitrogen and oxygen in the right proportion;
- the **CO₂ Scrubber** uses lithium hydroxide cartridges at first, which get used up, and later a regenerable zeolite
  bed, as on the International Space Station; the Sabatier Reactor can turn the carbon dioxide into water and methane;
- **plants**: leaves and crops inside a sealed room slowly turn carbon dioxide into oxygen, as Biosphere 2 (1991) and
  ESA's MELiSSA set out to do, so a greenhouse helps a room breathe;
- the **Atmosphere Detector**, J's Core's, shows the pressure and the composition.

**The airlock:** two pressurized doors with a small chamber between them and a **pump** that draws the chamber's air
back before the outer door opens. Without a pump, every passage loses the chamber's air. The Quest airlock of the
International Space Station works this way, and still loses a little.

**A room tells the player:** too little oxygen suffocates; too much carbon dioxide gives a headache and nausea first,
then danger (the International Space Station lives at about 0.4%, ten times the Earth's air, and above about 5% it is
dangerous); pure oxygen at high pressure poisons, slowly.

**Decompression.** If a wall breaks, or a door to the outside opens with no airlock, **the air rushes out in a jet**,
pulling items and players towards the hole, and the room stays in vacuum until it is patched and filled again. The only
people who ever died in space were the crew of Soyuz 11, in 1971, when a valve opened and their cabin emptied. A
consequence setting turns the pull off.

**A sealed room keeps its warmth**: it is always tempered, and the danger of heat and cold is for whoever goes out.

**The suitport.** A suit can stay docked on the outside wall of a base, and the player climbs into it from inside,
through its back as into an Orlan, so dust never comes in. It is a real NASA idea for rovers. The anti-dust module does
the same for a suit with no suitport.

### The dangers of every world

| Danger | Where | Without protection | What protects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Crushing pressure** | Venus (92 atm), Solum B (90), Fulgor | crushes | the hard suit |
| **Heat and cold** | Venus at 464 °C, Mercury's day side, the polar craters at -240 °C | burns or freezes | the right suit; a sealed room |
| **Poisonous air** | sulfur dioxide, methane and ammonia, **cyanogen** (Ithel I), carbon dioxide on Venus | poisons | a mask or a suit |
| **Cosmic radiation** | wherever there is no magnetic field or thick air, in the worlds not yet built | a dose that **builds up slowly** in whoever stays long | the radiation vest; **anti-radiation panels** over a home, of polyethylene, rich in hydrogen, the best real shield against cosmic rays (J's Core's radiation crosses blocks weakened by their material); the **lava tubes** are natural shelters |
| **Solar storms on the ground** | anywhere unsheltered in the inner Solar System | a strong dose in little time, **with a warning** | shelter: a home under anti-radiation panels, or the radiation vest. The storm of August 1972 struck between Apollo 16 and Apollo 17; had it caught a crew on the Moon, it would have been serious |
| **Radiation belts** | Io and Europa (lethal in minutes), the Van Allen belts when crossing them | a huge dose | heavy shielding; Apollo crossed the Van Allen belts in an hour |
| **Violent stars** | the pulsar Lich, the flares of Proxima and Cindara, the X-rays of Cygnus X-1 | an extreme dose | heavy shielding |
| **Strong fields** | Cindara II (50 times the Earth's field), Fulgor | instruments fail, and the player is shocked in Fulgor's fields of lightning | the anti-field module |
| **Lunar dust** | the Moon | fine and sharp, it comes indoors on the suit; Harrison Schmitt (Apollo 17) got "lunar hay fever" from it | the suitport; the anti-dust module |
| **Perchlorates** | the soil of Mars | poison whoever farms straight in it | washing the soil first |
| **Gravity** | every world | **a fall hurts by the local gravity**: on the Moon almost nothing, on LHS 1140 b (1.9 g) much more; jumps change too | the relativistic module |

The Moon and Mars list their dust, perchlorates, gravity and storms among their dangers as data, and the mechanics of
those four are still to build.

Radiation, heat, cold and air are J's Core's mechanics, its hazard event and its radiation of two channels; J's Space
only says how much of each every world has ([The universe](universe.md)).
