# Worlds

Every world in detail: its biomes, its landmarks, what makes it unlike any other, and, where there is life, the
creatures that live there. The conditions of every world, its generator and its sky are in [The universe](universe.md);
its minerals in [Minerals](minerals.md); its ruins and Enigmatic sites in [Structures](structures.md).

## To build

### What every world has

- **Biomes of its own**: no world borrows a biome from another.
- **Landmarks**: unique landforms with their names, real ones on real worlds, each placed once, **near the world's
  origin**, at a distance that can be crossed. A landmark shows on the Atlas once the relief of its region is known
  ([Exploration](exploration.md)), and visiting one is an advancement. Heights are squeezed into the world's height, and
  the proportions between them stay: Olympus Mons is the tallest of all.
- **Its weather, its sky and its minerals**, and, where there are, life and structures.

### Historic sites

The real probes and landers are on the worlds where they really are, as **small structures with a plaque** that tells
what each one was: the Apollo descent stages on the Moon, with the flag and the lunar rover; Lunokhod; the Venera
landers on Venus; the rovers on Mars, Opportunity silent since 2018, and Ingenuity; Huygens on Titan. Visiting one is an
advancement and gives **data** of that place, as the parts of Surveyor 3 that Apollo 12 brought home did.

**Probes in interstellar space.** The probes that left are where they really are, in the Solar System's space, still
moving out slowly:

| Probe | Where | Contact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voyager 1 | more than 160 AU | still talking |
| Voyager 2 | about 140 AU | still talking, only through the Canberra antenna, since it lies in the southern sky |
| Pioneer 10, Pioneer 11 | about 135 and 115 AU | silent since 2003 and 1995 |
| New Horizons | about 60 AU | active |

The ones still talking are **found by their signal**, with a large dish; the silent Pioneers are **searched for** with a
telescope at their predicted position (the "Pioneer anomaly", a tiny drift in their paths, was a mystery until 2012,
when it was shown to be their own heat pushing them). A starship **catches up with them** and takes them aboard, and
brought to the Space Research Computer they give **a great deal of research**: decades in the interstellar medium, and
what they carry, the **Golden Record** of the Voyagers (1977) and the Pioneers' **plaque**, with two people drawn on it
and a map of pulsars that shows where the Sun is. Every recovery is a milestone.

### Life

- On the worlds of **earthlike life** (Archaeon, Nereus, Arthon), animals are **analogues of real animals of the Earth's
  past, with their real names**. The manual says why: an animal of Nereus so like the Earth's *Anomalocaris* that
  scientists gave it the same name.
- On the worlds of **strange life** (Fulgor, Cindara II, Vorna C), the beings are the series' own, true to the chemistry
  of their world.
- **Models in Minecraft's style**, of blocks, with the real silhouettes; simple behaviour, as vanilla's mobs: passive,
  neutral or hostile.
- **Life is one more property of a world** to discover ([Exploration](exploration.md)).
- **No drop makes farming them worth it.** A creature gives a **specimen** for science and a trophy, never a material;
  and a specimen gives science only the first few times for each species. Studying pays; killing in series doesn't.
- **A creature can live on another world if the air there suits it**, which includes a sealed room with the right air
  ([Life support](life-support.md)).
- **Creatures aren't tamed.** Ways to tame some, coherent with each one, may come later.

**Terminus.** Its landmarks are named after **the edges of the known world**: the planet is the frontier.

- **Biomes:** **glaciers of nitrogen** that flow, with convection cells; **seas of oxygen** in the warmed basins, with
  icebergs of nitrogen; **volcanic provinces** of basalt rich in KREEP, with lava boiling the ice around it; **plains of
  frozen oxygen**; highlands of methane frost; lowlands stained with red tholin.
- **Landmarks:** **Mare Tenebrosum**, the largest sea of oxygen ("the Sea of Darkness" was the medieval name of the
  Atlantic beyond the known world); **the Pillars of Hercules**, two peaks at the mouth of the sea (the pillars of the
  Strait of Gibraltar, where *non plus ultra*, "nothing beyond", was written); **Ultima Thule Mons**, the greatest
  volcano (Ultima Thule was the farthest north of the ancients' world, and Arrokoth's nickname before 2019); **Limes
  Rupes**, a scarp that cuts across the planet like the frontier of the Roman Empire; the **farthest human outpost**,
  with an unfinished starship in its dock ([Structures](structures.md)).
- **Its own:** **oxygen is pale blue**, liquid or solid, which is real: blue seas and plains under a black sky, with the
  Sun shining like about 110 full moons.

**Cardea**, the shipyard: flat, firm plains of ice, CO₂ frost, dark patches; **Ianua Planitia** ("door"), a smooth basin
that is a **natural landing field**; the crater **Cardo** ("hinge").

**Limentinus**, the geysers: **fields of geysers** at the south pole, as on Enceladus; ridged plains; **Limen Fossae**
("threshold"), the fissures the geysers rise from; overhead, the **ring of ice** they feed.

**Doris**, the ocean. Its landmarks are named after the **Nereids**, Doris' daughters: a shell of ice with **chaos
terrain**; ice volcanoes of water and ammonia; smooth plains; **under the ice, the ocean and its hydrothermal vents**.
**Galene Planitia** (Galene, of the calm sea); **Amphitrite Patera**, the greatest ice volcano; **Kymo Chaos** (Kymo, of
the waves).

**Acheron**, the dark one. Its landmarks are named after **the rivers of the underworld**: plains of **black dust**;
ancient craters; fresh craters showing **white ice** beneath, bright spots on black. **Lethe Valles** (the river of
forgetting); the crater **Cocytus**; and the **observatory site**, the coldest and quietest place in the Solar System.

**Eurynome**, the stranger: untouched ice and very red tholin; **Ophion Dorsum**, a long, winding ridge like a serpent
(Ophion, the serpent who ruled with Eurynome before both fell into Ocean); the **Enigmatic site**
([Structures](structures.md)).

**Archaeon**, the Silurian (about 430 million years ago), when life began to come out of the water:

- **Biomes:** **plains of Prototaxites**, towers up to 8 m of a giant organism (probably a fungus; some say not even
  that), over mats of moss and liverworts; **primitive shores** with the first true plants, the **Cooksonia**, a few
  centimetres tall; swamps; bare rock; **reefs of tabulate and rugose corals**, the extinct corals of that age; shallow,
  temperate and abyssal seas.
- **In the sea:** **trilobites** (passive); **graptolites**, floating colonies (passive); **sea lilies**, crinoids fixed
  to the floor (passive); **straight-shelled nautiloids**, shells several metres long (neutral); **eurypterids**, the
  sea scorpions of 1.6 m such as *Pterygotus* (**hostile**); shoals of **jawless fish** (passive, food).
- **On land:** **millipedes**, such as *Pneumodesmus*, the oldest known animal to breathe air (passive). Nothing else:
  the land is almost empty.

**Nereus**, the Cambrian (about 510 million years ago), the explosion of life, all of it in the sea:

- **Biomes:** **plains of the auroras**, barren land with mats of microbes at the edges; **reefs of archaeocyathids**,
  the first reefs built by animals; a shallow Cambrian sea, full of life; the open sea; the abyss.
- **In the sea:** **trilobites** everywhere (passive); ***Anomalocaris***, the predator of 1 m with claws before its
  mouth (**hostile**); ***Opabinia***, with five eyes and a clawed trunk (passive); ***Hallucigenia***, a spiny worm
  scientists drew **upside down** until 1991 (passive); ***Wiwaxia***, covered in scales (passive); ***Marrella***, the
  commonest animal of the Burgess Shale (passive); jellyfish (neutral, they sting); and ***Pikaia***, a little creature
  that is **a distant relative of every vertebrate**, us included (passive).
- **On land:** nothing.

**Fulgor**, the world of electric life. Cygnus X-1 is **the first black hole science believed in**, 21 solar masses,
circling a blue supergiant every 5.6 days (in 1974 Stephen Hawking bet it wasn't a black hole, and paid Kip Thorne in
1990). Fulgor orbits the pair from afar.

- **Its sky:** the **blue supergiant**, large and blue-white, and beside it the black hole's **disc**, a glowing ring
  that **pulses**, with its **jets**, real ones that blew a bubble of gas around it, found in 2005. The pulse is
  Fulgor's cycle of light, and the disc's **bursts of X-rays** are the danger of the place, with a warning.
- **The world:** about 200 °C, thick air and a "sea" of **supercritical CO₂**, neither liquid nor gas; **onnesite** in
  the cracks.
- **Biomes:** **discharge plains**, with lightning that **rises from the ground to the sky**; **electric forests**,
  "trees" of stable plasma glowing in patterns that aren't random; **plasma deltas**, rivers of light flowing slowly;
  **plasma arches**, natural bridges of light between rocks; **electron fog**, where almost nothing can be seen; the
  **supercritical sea**, whose haze bends the disc's light into impossible colours; **discharge shores**; the
  high-pressure **depths**.
- **Beings**, the series' own: **electric orbs**, drifting with the field's currents (passive); **colonies of orbs**
  moving in step (passive); **predatory filaments**, which stretch out suddenly to strike (**hostile**); **plasma
  serpents** in the sea (**hostile**, very fast); **electric jellyfish** (neutral, they shock); and the **electron
  humanoids**, with their cities and **guardians** ([Structures](structures.md)).

**Arthon**, the Permian, **the age of synapsids**, the ancient kin of mammals that ruled the land before the dinosaurs:

- **Biomes:** savanna of ferns and **Glossopteris** (the "seed fern" that covered the south of the Earth); swamps of
  **peat and forming coal**; dry plateaus; deltas; **reefs** of rugose corals; shallow and open seas. The world stands
  **on the brink of the Great Dying**: at the end of the Permian, 252 million years ago, the volcanoes of the Siberian
  Traps heated the Earth and killed about 90% of the species of the sea. Arthon has its **province of giant volcanoes**,
  its landmark, hot (35 °C), with acid rain near them.
- **On land:** ***Dimetrodon***, the sail on its back, closer kin to mammals than to dinosaurs (**hostile**);
  **gorgonopsians** with sabre teeth, such as *Inostrancevia* of 3.5 m (**hostile**, the top predator); **dicynodonts**
  in herds, such as *Lystrosaurus*, the survivor of the great extinction (passive); armoured **pareiasaurs**, such as
  *Scutosaurus* (neutral); ***Meganeuropsis***, the dragonfly with a **wingspan of 71 cm**, the largest insect that ever
  lived (neutral); ***Arthropleura***, the millipede of **2.6 m** (neutral).
- **In the sea:** ***Helicoprion***, the shark with a **spiral of teeth** in its jaw (**hostile**); ammonites and
  brachiopods (passive).

**Cindara II**, the world of magnetic life:

- **Biomes:** **magnetic forests**, columns of magnetite that attract and repel each other; plains and valleys with
  **the pattern of the field** drawn on the ground; peaks of ferrite; **arches** of magnetite with no visible support;
  **ferrite fog**; fields of giant **polar crystals** of cindarite (10 blocks and more); plains of magnetic storms with
  auroras; **the sea of ferrofluid**, whose surface rises in **spikes** as the field passes (the real effect is called
  the Rosensweig instability, seen in laboratories with magnets and ferrofluid); bays of magnetite; the polar abyss.
- **The base of its life** is real: **magnetotactic bacteria**, which build chains of magnetite inside themselves to
  steer by the field, have been known on the Earth since 1975. On Cindara II, all life grew from them.
- **Beings**, the series' own: **magnetic spheres**, which tug at the player's iron items, only in appearance (passive);
  **colonies** in formation (passive); **domain predators**, which glide and drive the compass mad up close
  (**hostile**); **iron currents**, which swim in the sea like fish (passive); **ferromagnetic vortices**, which pull
  the player and their metal (**hostile**); the **magnetic humanoids**, their cities and guardians
  ([Structures](structures.md)).
- **Dangers:** a field 50 times the Earth's (the anti-field module); the red dwarf's flares.

**Vorna C**, the world of silicon life:

- **Biomes:** **crystal forests**, beings that look like quartz and feldspar; **chemical plains**, a garden of almost
  motionless "living stones"; **reducing fissures** of hydrogen and hydrogen sulfide, yellow with sulfur, where life is
  densest; **plains of vornaite**, the deposits of the dead; reducing caves; transition zones; shores, sea and floor of
  **ammonia** in the cold regions.
- **Beings**, the series' own: **at first sight the world looks like geology**, and only by watching longer does the
  player see the crystals **move**, slowly, and answer them. **Crystal bushes** (passive, they react to the vibration of
  footsteps); **silicon crawlers** (neutral); **crystalline predators**, faster, which **spit silica** (**hostile**);
  colonies floating in the hydrogen air (passive); **water crystals** in the ammonia (passive); ammonia **devourers**,
  which dissolve smaller beings (**hostile**).
- **The real thing:** silicon life is a real speculation. Silicon forms chains as carbon does, but they are weak in
  water and oxygen; **in an ammonia solvent, with no oxygen, it would have its best chance**, which is exactly Vorna C's
  world.
