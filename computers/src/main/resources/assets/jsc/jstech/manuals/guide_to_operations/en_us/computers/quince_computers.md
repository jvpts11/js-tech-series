# Quince's computers

Quince makes its own computers, from the Russet 128K of the Vintage to the coloured qRusset of the Advanced. Each is a Personal Computer in every way but its case: you install a system on it, run programs and reach the network from it ([](jsc:personal_computers)).

Table: Quince's computers by era

| Era | All-in-ones | Towers and the Studio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vintage | Russet 128K | None |
| Legacy | qRusset G3, in six flavours | Russet Power G4 |
| Transition | qRusset (White) | Russet Pro (Transition) |
| Standard | qRusset (Slim) | Russet Pro (Standard) |
| Advanced | qRusset, in seven colours | Russet Studio |

Quince's hardware is closed. Each machine takes only its own Quince Logic Board, and that board goes in no other case. The memory, the disks and the cards are the common ones. The Russet Pros take the Quince Heat Sink of their era over the processor.

An all-in-one carries its screen in its own block. Its glass shows what the machine shows, as a monitor does: use the glass to sit down at it, and use the case anywhere else to open its parts. Its sound comes from its own case.

The all-in-ones and the Studio take their cables only from behind, and their power button is behind them too. A tower takes cables as a Personal Computer does, its power button is on its front, and sneaking as you use it takes its left side off.

Switch a Quince machine on while you sneak, and it stops at its start-up choices before it starts: Open Firmware's prompt on the Legacy (type quince-boot to go on, or shut-down to switch it off), the Startup Manager on the Transition and the Standard, and the Startup Options on the Advanced. Pick a disk with the arrow keys and press Enter. The Russet 128K has no choices.

## If something goes wrong

> [!PROBLEM] A motherboard will not go in.
> A Quince machine takes only its own Quince Logic Board.

> [!PROBLEM] The screen shows the Sad Quince.
> The parts do not make a computer: the line under it says what is missing, such as a processor or memory. Switch the machine off and put the part in.

> [!PROBLEM] The machine stays at the Startup Manager.
> You switched it on while you sneaked. Pick a disk and press Enter.
