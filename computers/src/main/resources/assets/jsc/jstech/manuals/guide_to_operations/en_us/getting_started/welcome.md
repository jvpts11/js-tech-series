# Welcome to J's Computers

J's Computers is about computers. But what is a computer here? You might expect a box that you place and switch on. It is that, but it is also built part by part, the way a real one is.

Computers that are joined by data cable to one Mainframe ([](jsc:mainframes)) make a network ([](jsc:what_a_network_is)). The network keeps your items on its disks, moves them, and crafts what you ask for.

When a computer starts, the first thing it runs is the firmware ([](jsc:firmware)): the program that checks the hardware and finds the disks. Then it hands over to an operating system, the system you use day to day.

To build a working computer you need parts: a motherboard, a processor (CPU), memory (RAM) and a power supply (PSU). A disk holds the system, and a graphics card (GPU) gives the monitor a picture. Each part has its own entry, and [Building your first computer](jsc:first_computer) puts them together.

In survival you make every part along the mod's own chain, from the ore you dig to the Electronics Workbench of its era ([](jsc:fab_from_ore)), and the recipe viewer, JEI or EMI, shows how each thing is made. In creative, the parts are on the creative tabs: J's Computers holds what every era shares, and there is one tab for each era, from J's Computers - Vintage to J's Computers - Advanced ([](jsc:eras)).

## How to read this manual

Every entry starts on a page of its own. The row of items under an entry's title, the plate, is clickable: click an item to go to its own page. A number in brackets is a link to another entry.

Hold the manual key over an item and the manual opens at the item's page. The manual key is [[M]] unless you changed it.

The Search button finds anything in the manual by name. A search that finds nothing says "Nothing by that name".

The buttons Contents, Index and Glossary, and Back and Forward, move you around the book.

This book, the Guide to Operations, is one of seven books. The other six are the Frames Resource Kit, The Linux Desk Reference, Linux by Hand, The Daemon Handbook, Learning IQL and The Book of Sigma. Each book is an item in the J's Computers creative tab, and the library ([](jsc:library)) says what each one covers.

> [!DEFINE] Firmware
> The program on the motherboard that starts the computer. It is the first thing that runs when you turn the computer on.

> [!DEFINE] Operating system
> The system a computer starts into, and what every program runs on. MC-DOS, Frames and the Linux distributions are operating systems.

> [!DEFINE] Era
> A generation of hardware, such as Vintage or Standard. Parts of one era are built to fit the boards of that era.
