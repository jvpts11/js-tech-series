# The Start menu of each edition

The Start menu is where you find the programs of your Frames. Click the Start button on the taskbar, and the menu opens above it. The general idea is on the page [Desktops](jsc:desktops).

Each Frames edition draws its Start menu in its own style. Every one of them lists your programs, and each has a power control at its foot.

Table: The Start menus

| Edition | Start button | What the menu holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frames 95 | Start, at the left corner of the taskbar | One vertical list of programs, under a side band that carries the name of the system. |
| Frames XP | A green pill labelled "start" | Two columns: programs on the left, places on the right. |
| Frames 7 | A round glass button at the left end | Two columns in a frame of glass: programs on the white side, places on the glass side. |
| Frames 10 | Start, beside the search box | A rail, a list of programs and a set of tiles. |
| Frames 11 | Centred with the program icons | A floating panel: a search box, a grid of pinned programs and a power button. |

## Frames 95 and Frames XP

The menu of Frames 95 is a single vertical list, one row for each program. Below the programs, a row labelled "Shut Down" opens the [power controls](jsc:shutting_down).

Frames XP splits its menu into two columns. The left column lists your programs, and the first two are in bold, because they are pinned. The right column holds the places: This PC, Files, Settings and Network. The header shows your own name, and the footer holds "Log Off" and "Turn Off Computer".

## Frames 7

The menu of Frames 7 is drawn in glass. Its left column is white and lists your programs, with the first two in bold. A search box at the foot of that column filters the list as you type.

The right column is the glass itself. At its head is the picture of your account, and under it are the places in white: Files, Computer, Network, Settings and Device Manager. Frames 7 calls This PC "Computer". "Shut down" sits at the foot of the column.

The menu opens over the corner of the screen, level with the top of the bar. The round button stays in the bar beneath it.

## Frames 10

The menu of Frames 10 has three parts. The narrow rail at the left holds the menu's own button at the top. At the foot of the rail are your account, your files, Settings and the power button.

Beside the rail is the list. It shows the programs you used most in this session, up to 4 of them, under the heading "Most used". Then it lists every program from A to Z under its letter. When you type in the search box, the list keeps only what matches.

The tiles at the right of the menu are squares, each standing for one program. The tile sizes are these:

Table: Tile sizes

| Size | Cells | Menu text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 1 by 1 cell | Resize: Small |
| Medium | 2 by 2 cells | Resize: Medium |
| Wide | 4 by 2 cells | Resize: Wide |

A Frames 10 machine keeps up to 24 tiles. A new machine starts with File Explorer, Calculator, System Monitor, Settings, Editor, Command Prompt, Paint, Minesweeper and Network Manager on its tiles.

Two tiles are live. The Network Manager tile reads "Connected to a network" or "No network". The System Monitor tile shows the processor in use, marked "CPU" (central processing unit), and the memory in use, marked "Memory". A live tile turns over every 7 seconds, between its picture and its reading.

You arrange the tiles with the mouse. A right click on a tile unpins it or changes its size. A right click on a program in the list pins it, with "Pin to Start". Drag a tile onto another tile to take its place.

## Frames 11

Frames 11 uses a compact floating panel. It has a search box that reads "Type here to search", a grid of pinned programs under the heading "Pinned", and a footer. The account is at the left of the footer, and the power button at the right. The grid is 5 columns wide.

## Using the Start menu

Whatever the edition, the search box starts empty each time you open the menu. The search keeps the programs whose name contains the text you typed, and capital letters do not matter. It takes up to 24 characters.

A left click starts the program you point at. A right click opens that program's own menu.

1. Click the Start button in the taskbar.
2. Find the program in the list, or type part of its name.
3. Click the program with the left button.

## If something goes wrong

> [!PROBLEM] The menu says "No results" while you type.
> Check the spelling, or clear the search box and look through the list.

> [!PROBLEM] You want a 25th tile on a Frames 10 menu.
> A machine keeps up to 24 tiles. Right-click a tile, choose "Unpin from Start", then pin the new program.

> [!DEFINE] desktop
> The face a system shows after it starts: the wallpaper, the windows, the taskbar and the Start menu.

> [!DEFINE] Start menu
> The menu that opens from the Start button, with your programs, the places of the system and the power controls.

> [!DEFINE] taskbar
> The bar along the bottom of the screen, with the Start button, a button for each open program and the status area.

> [!DEFINE] window
> A box on the desktop that holds one open program.

> [!DEFINE] tile
> A square on the Frames 10 Start menu that stands for one program, and starts that program when you click it.

> [!DEFINE] pin
> Keeping a program on a tile, a square in the grid or a button on the taskbar, so that it stays there when the program is closed.
