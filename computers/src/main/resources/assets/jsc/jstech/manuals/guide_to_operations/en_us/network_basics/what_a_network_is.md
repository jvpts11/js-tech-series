# What a network is here

J's Computers has no channels and no subnetworks at all. A network here works much like a real computer network.

A network is every computer and device joined by data cable to one Mainframe, its brain ([](jsc:mainframes)). The cables come in lines, by the job they do: the access line joins the small computers to a router, the backbone joins the routers, the Mainframe and the racks, the long distance line joins two networks, gateway to gateway, and the high compute line joins a supercomputer's nodes ([](jsc:data_cables)). You lay the line the job asks for, and you never count what a cable holds.

What a cable does have is a speed and a range: how many items per tick an Operation crossing it can move, and how many cables a run can be before a router or a repeater renews it. So a network built with the right cables in the right places is a fast network.

And what does the network do for you? It keeps your items as data on its servers' disks ([](jsc:items_as_data)), gives them back when you ask, and crafts what you need ([](jsc:how_autocrafting_works)). Every one of those is an Operation ([](jsc:what_operations_are)).

> [!DEFINE] Network
> Every computer and device joined by data cable to one Mainframe, which runs the network.

> [!DEFINE] Data cable
> A cable that carries the data of a network, in one of the lines of the data cables.

> [!DEFINE] Router
> A block with six faces that joins the access line of an era to its backbone, and extends the network it is part of.

> [!DEFINE] Repeater
> A block laid in a run of cable that renews the run's range, and carries each line on.

> [!DEFINE] Speed
> The most items per tick that an Operation crossing a cable can move.

> [!DEFINE] Range
> How many cables a run can be before a router or a repeater renews it. A run longer than its range carries nothing.
