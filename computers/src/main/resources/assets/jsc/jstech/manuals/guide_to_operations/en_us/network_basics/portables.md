# Portables: PDAs, smartphones and tablets

A portable is a computer you carry: a PDA in the Legacy and the Transition, a smartphone from the Transition on, a tablet from the Standard on. It joins the network of the nearest access point that reaches you, at the speed of the older of the two, as a computer with a Wi-Fi card does ([](jsc:wireless)). Use it to hold it up: its own face frames its screen, and you press its screen and its buttons with the mouse.

> [!DEFINE] Portable
> A PDA, a smartphone or a tablet: a computer you carry, with its own system from the factory.

> [!DEFINE] Mobile Device Charging Dock
> The dock of an era that holds one portable of its era and charges its battery.

## Its system

Each portable runs its system from the factory, in the look of its years: Frames Pocket on the PDAs, qOS on Quince's qPhones and qPads, Droidix on the others. Switched on, it shows its maker's mark, then its system starting, then its lock. You let the lock go with its slider, a swipe up, or the Unlock key, as your system has it. Its screen shows the world's time and the day as Day N.

The network's alerts reach your portable and wait where its system keeps them: on Frames Pocket's Today and in a balloon, on the lock and in the Notification Center you pull down on qOS, on the lock and in the shade you pull down on Droidix; the Alerts icon counts the ones you have not seen. Its settings show its hardware, its Wi-Fi and its battery.

Its apps come from its store, the Frames Marketplace, the Quince Store or the Droidix Store, which the network's Mirror serves; everything in it is free. The network app and the alerts come with every portable, and the camera, the gallery and Maps with the smartphones and the tablets; the Crypto Wallet installs from the Transition on, the Messenger and prospecting from the Standard on.

## Its apps

The network app is a smaller Network Interactor. Stock lists the network's items; pick one and Request puts it into your portable's own storage, never straight into your inventory. Device shows what that storage holds: Take brings it to your inventory, Return gives it back, and Deposit lists your inventory row by row. Craft makes what the network can make, plans it first if you like, and shows the jobs running. A portable holds what its storage holds over the item size of its era's disk, less its system: 4 items on the Hightide Pocket, 112 items on the Standard's qPhone, about a thousand items on the qPad Pro.

The Alerts app chooses which of the network's five kinds of alert reach your portable, and the items it watches, each with the level below which it tells you. Turn on Alerts on the HUD in your client settings to see them on your screen too.

The camera takes a picture of what you see, without the HUD, at its era's resolution; the gallery keeps it in the portable's storage and sends it to a personal computer of the network as a PNG file in its pictures folder. Maps draws the land round you with the network's Mainframe, its access points and their reach; prospecting shows which way the veins near you lie and how strongly, never where. The Messenger talks to the network's own Messenger service.

While you carry a portable on a network, you build from that network's storage too: a building tool takes from it what your inventory lacks, and the Multiblock Builder takes from it when you choose Network storage as its source.

## Its battery

A portable runs on its battery while you carry it: about two game days on a smartphone or a tablet, a day and a half on a PDA. Its battery is the bar under its icon, and its tooltip says its share. Flat, it switches off and leaves the network.

Stand it in the Mobile Device Charging Dock of its era with a right click, and take it out with an empty hand; the dock has no window. The dock takes its energy from behind and charges the battery at its charger's watts, using only what it gives the battery. While it stands there the portable is off, its screen showing the battery filling, and the dock's lamp is lit; out of the dock it starts again.

## If something goes wrong

> [!PROBLEM] Your portable says No access point in reach.
> Walk within an access point's reach, or set one on the network near you.

> [!PROBLEM] Its store installs nothing.
> The network's Mainframe has to run the Mirror; a portable off the network cannot reach it.

> [!PROBLEM] The dock will not take your portable.
> A dock holds a portable of its own era alone: use the dock of the portable's era.

> [!PROBLEM] A portable lost its apps and its pictures.
> Its item was destroyed, or it lay out of every inventory and dock longer than the server allows: what was on it is gone.
