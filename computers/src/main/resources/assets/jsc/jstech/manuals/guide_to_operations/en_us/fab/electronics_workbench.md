# The Electronics Workbench

Everything electronic in a computer starts at an Electronics Workbench, one for each era from the Vintage to the Advanced. A bench assembles the parts of its era and of every era before it, and the bench of the next era. It is your own bench, used by hand: nothing reaches its trays through its sides, and no Crafting Interface works it.

> [!DEFINE] Electronics Workbench
> The bench of an era where the chain's parts are assembled by hand, from the ingredients laid in its nine trays.

Lay each ingredient the part asks for in a tray of its own, in any tray and any order, with at least the count the part takes, and leave nothing else in the trays. The part shows faded beside the trays once the bench knows it, and the groove fills in the colour of the era's tool as it comes.

The bench works as a furnace does: each part takes its time, and you may close its window meanwhile. The finished part waits in the part's tray until you take it, and the bench starts the next while the trays still hold enough.

It draws energy through the socket on its back and nowhere else, while it works. The power strip on its window has a lamp lit while energy reaches the bench, what it draws, and the energy it holds. Each era's parts are a kind of recipe of their own, which recipe viewers show with the benches that make them.

## If something goes wrong

> [!PROBLEM] The bench does not start.
> A tray holds something the part does not take, an ingredient is short of its count, the part is of a later era than the bench, or no energy reaches its back.

> [!PROBLEM] The bench stopped with the part half done.
> The part's tray is full: take the finished parts out.

> [!PROBLEM] A hopper puts nothing in the bench.
> It takes nothing through its sides: lay the ingredients in by hand.
